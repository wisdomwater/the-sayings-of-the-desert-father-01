## Day 31 — Our Life Is With Our Neighbor

### Abba Anthony

Abba Anthony said that our life and our death are bound up with our neighbor. If we gain our brother or sister, we gain God. If we wound or scandalize them, we sin against Christ.

Anthony refuses to let spirituality become private. Our prayer, solitude, discipline, and inner life are not separate from the way we speak, react, forgive, and make room for another person. The neighbor becomes one of the places where our relationship with God is revealed.

### Devotional

It is possible to imagine spiritual life as something that happens mostly between the individual and God. We pray, read, meditate, examine our conscience, and try to become more attentive. All of that matters, but Anthony places another person directly in the middle of the path.

That can be inconvenient.

Other people interrupt the quiet. They misunderstand us, disappoint us, need things at the wrong time, and expose reactions we would rather believe we had outgrown. Solitude may make us feel peaceful until someone enters the room.

Anthony does not treat this interruption as a distraction from spiritual life. The relationship itself becomes part of the practice. The person who irritates us may reveal more about the state of our heart than an hour of undisturbed prayer.

This does not mean that every relationship must be close. Boundaries can be wise, distance can be necessary, and reconciliation does not always mean restored intimacy. But even when distance is needed, contempt does not have to govern the heart.

To “gain” our neighbor may therefore mean something deeper than winning their approval. It may mean refusing to reduce them to the worst thing they have done, or refusing to let conflict turn them into an enemy in our imagination. We preserve the possibility of seeing a person rather than merely an offense.

Judgment tends to flatten.

Once we decide what someone is, everything they do begins confirming the verdict. Their motives become obvious to us, their failures become predictable, and their complexity disappears. Mercy restores depth.

Mercy does not deny harm. It simply refuses to believe that harm tells the whole story of a human being. It leaves room for history, weakness, fear, ignorance, change, repentance, and grace.

Anthony’s saying also turns the question back toward us. We may be very concerned with whether another person is wrong while paying less attention to what our response is making of us. Their behavior matters, but so does the condition of our own heart.

This is where judgment becomes dangerous.

We can be factually correct and still become cruel. We can identify another person’s failure accurately while allowing resentment, superiority, or contempt to take root in us. Being right does not automatically mean being rightly related.

Anthony links the neighbor with Christ because love cannot remain abstract. We may say that we love God, but eventually that love must take flesh in the presence of someone concrete. The test often comes not with the person we naturally love, but with the one who makes love difficult.

That does not always require a dramatic gesture. Sometimes gaining a neighbor means listening before answering, refusing a cutting remark, telling the truth without humiliation, or choosing not to rehearse someone’s failure for the hundredth time. Small movements can keep relationship from hardening into enmity.

We will not do this perfectly. Relationships are too complicated, and we are too reactive for that. But perhaps the work is simply to keep noticing whenever another person has become less than human in our mind.

Then we can return.

We can remember that this person also carries fear, longing, wounds, hopes, and a life hidden from us. We can remember that Christ does not meet us only in silence or sacrament, but also in the difficult presence of another human being. Our life with God and our life with one another begin to look less like two paths and more like the same path seen from different sides.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Let me see my neighbor clearly.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me the way of mercy.*
