## Day 16 — Stay Awake to God

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John said that watchfulness means remaining in the cell and continually remembering God. The cell teaches us to stay, while remembrance teaches us what we are staying for. Stillness is not emptiness for its own sake, but a way of becoming available to what is already present.

John does not describe watchfulness as anxious searching. He does not tell us to strain for some extraordinary experience. He invites us to remain present long enough to notice that God is already near.

### Devotional

The word *watchfulness* can sound tense, as though we are supposed to stand guard over ourselves and scan constantly for danger. There is certainly something vigilant in the desert tradition, because the Fathers and Mothers paid close attention to thoughts, impulses, and the subtle movements of the heart. But John points toward something gentler as well.

For him, watching is remembering God. That changes the atmosphere of the practice, because we are no longer simply guarding against what might go wrong. We are learning to remain awake to what is already here.

Much of life happens while our attention is somewhere else. We eat while thinking about work, work while anticipating the evening, sit with someone while composing our next response, and lie in bed replaying yesterday or rehearsing tomorrow. The body inhabits one moment while the mind lives in another.

John’s cell becomes a school of presence. Stay here, he seems to say, and remember God here. Not the God you encountered yesterday or the God you hope to find on some future retreat, but the God of this actual moment.

This does not require us to produce constant religious thoughts. Remembering God may be as simple as returning to reality: feeling the breath enter the body, listening to the person speaking, hearing the rain, washing the dish in our hand, or allowing silence to remain unfilled. These ordinary acts can become ways of coming back to where we already are.

The contemplative traditions have often treated forgetfulness as one of our deepest spiritual problems. We forget where we are, who we are, and that the ordinary moment is already unfolding within something larger than ourselves. Then, every so often, we remember.

Nothing outside us may change when that happens. The room is the same, the problem remains unresolved, the body still aches, and the work still waits. Yet awareness opens, and the moment is no longer merely something to get through.

We are here.

And God is here.

Perhaps this is why John connects watchfulness with staying in the cell. Continually running elsewhere makes remembrance difficult, because something in us has to become willing to remain long enough for the surface agitation to settle. Only then do we begin to notice what distraction kept hidden.

What we notice may not be dramatic. It may be a face, a breath, a moment of quiet, or a life that was happening while we were busy thinking about something else. Watchfulness is the gentle discipline of waking up to it.

Again and again, we return to the present and discover that presence has been waiting for us there.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *You are here.*

**Breathing out:** *Let me be here too.*
