## Day 16 — Stay Awake to God

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John said that watchfulness means remaining in the cell and continually remembering God.

The cell teaches us to stay.

The remembering teaches us what to stay for.

We do not become watchful by anxiously searching for something extraordinary.

We become watchful by remaining present long enough to notice that God is already near.

### Devotional

The word *watchfulness* can sound tense. It may bring to mind someone standing guard through the night, scanning the darkness for danger. There is certainly something vigilant in the desert tradition. The Fathers and Mothers paid close attention to thoughts, impulses, and the subtle ways the heart could become distracted. But John's saying points in another direction too. Watching is remembering God.

This changes the atmosphere completely. We are not simply guarding against what might go wrong. We are learning to remain awake to what is already here.

Much of life happens while our attention is somewhere else. We eat while thinking about work. We work while anticipating the evening. We sit with someone while composing our next response. We lie in bed replaying yesterday or rehearsing tomorrow. The body occupies one moment while the mind lives in another. John's cell becomes a school of presence. Stay here. Remember God here.

Not the God you encountered yesterday. Not the God you hope to experience on retreat. Not the God hidden behind some future version of yourself who is finally disciplined enough to pray properly.

God here.

This does not require constant religious thought. Remembering God may sometimes be no more complicated than returning to reality. Feel the breath entering the body. Notice the person speaking. Hear the rain. Wash the dish that is actually in your hand. Allow the silence to remain silent.

The contemplative traditions have often understood forgetfulness as one of our deepest spiritual problems. We forget where we are. We forget who we are. We forget that the ordinary moment is already taking place within something larger than ourselves. Then, occasionally, we remember.

Nothing outside us may change. The room is the same. The problem remains unresolved. The body still aches. The work still waits. But awareness opens, and the moment is no longer merely something to get through.

We are here. And God is here. Perhaps this is why John connects watchfulness with staying in the cell. Continually running elsewhere makes remembrance difficult. Something in us has to become willing to remain long enough for the surface agitation to settle.

Then we begin to see. Not necessarily visions or revelations. Just reality itself, received with greater attention. A face. A breath. A moment of quiet. A life that was happening while we were busy thinking about something else. Watchfulness is the gentle discipline of waking up to it. Again and again.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *You are here.*

**Breathing out:** *Let me be here too.*
