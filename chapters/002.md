# Day 2 — Until the Last Breath

### Abba Anthony the Great

Abba Anthony once spoke with Abba Poemen about the great work of the spiritual life.

He told him to keep his own failures before God rather than placing them elsewhere. There would always be reasons to excuse himself, someone else to blame, some circumstance that could explain what had happened. Anthony urged him instead to remain willing to see himself truthfully before God.

And he told him not to imagine that temptation would someday disappear.

The struggle would accompany him until his final breath.

### Devotional

There is something almost disappointing about Anthony's words. We might hope that after enough prayer, enough wisdom, enough years of practice, we would finally arrive somewhere beyond temptation. Surely maturity should eventually mean that the old desires stop appearing, the old fears lose their voices, and the familiar weaknesses cease knocking at the door.

Anthony offers no such promise.

He seems to imagine the spiritual life not as reaching a place where struggle disappears, but as learning how to meet struggle differently. Temptation may remain, but we do not have to be surprised by it. The return of an old thought does not mean that everything we have practiced has failed. Perhaps maturity is revealed precisely in our growing ability to recognize the thought, receive the moment honestly, and choose again.

Anthony joins this with another practice: taking responsibility for our own sins before God. This can sound harsh if we hear it through the language of shame. But he is not asking us to declare ourselves worthless. He is asking us to stop spending our energy constructing explanations for why our failures belong to someone else.

There is freedom in this kind of honesty.

We can acknowledge that someone hurt us without pretending we had no choice in how we responded. We can recognize circumstances that made something difficult without using those circumstances to avoid looking at ourselves. Before God, there is no need for either prosecution or defense. There is simply the truth of what happened, held within mercy.

Perhaps these two teachings belong together. If we expect temptation to disappear, every recurrence becomes evidence that something is wrong with us. We become ashamed that we are still struggling with something we thought we should have conquered years ago. Shame then makes honesty harder, and we begin hiding precisely what needs to be brought into the light.

Anthony offers another way.

Expect the struggle. When you stumble, tell the truth. Then return.

The goal is not to become a person who never again feels anger, desire, envy, fear, resentment, or the pull of an old habit. The goal is to become increasingly free in their presence. They may knock at the door until our last breath, but knocking is not the same thing as entering.

There may never be a final morning when we wake beyond the need to choose. Yet perhaps this is not bad news. It means that every moment remains alive with possibility. Until the last breath, there is another opportunity to notice, another opportunity to tell the truth, another opportunity to return.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *I meet this moment honestly.*

**Breathing out:** *God, help me choose again.*
