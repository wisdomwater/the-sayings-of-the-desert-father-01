## Day 10 — A New Foundation

### Abba Silvanus

Abba Moses once asked Abba Silvanus a simple question.

“Can a person lay a new foundation every day?”

Silvanus answered him:

“If he is willing to do the work, he can lay a new foundation at every moment.”

The beginning is not limited to morning.

It is not limited to tomorrow.

The next moment is enough.

### Devotional

We often imagine change beginning at clean edges.

Monday morning. The first day of the month. A new year. A retreat. A fresh notebook. Tomorrow, when we are rested and prepared, we will finally begin living differently.

Silvanus takes away that excuse.

You do not need another day.

You need another moment.

There is something both demanding and merciful in this. It is demanding because we can no longer postpone transformation until conditions become ideal. But it is merciful because no amount of wasted time can prevent us from beginning now.

Perhaps the morning went badly.

You woke with good intentions and abandoned them before breakfast. You said something you regret. You lost yourself in distraction. You returned to the habit you had promised yourself you would resist. By noon, the temptation is to declare the day ruined.

We do this surprisingly often.

Once the imagined perfect day has been broken, we surrender the rest of it. We tell ourselves that tomorrow will be the real beginning.

Silvanus refuses to give failure that much power.

A new foundation can be laid at noon. Or at 2:17 in the afternoon. Or in the middle of the argument, when you suddenly realize what is happening. Or halfway through the meal. Or while reaching for the thing you had decided you did not want. Or in the silence immediately after words leave your mouth and you wish you could take them back.

The next moment remains available.

This does not mean that consequences disappear. A new foundation does not erase what has already happened. Sometimes we need to apologize, repair something, change a pattern, or accept the result of an earlier choice.

But the past does not get to own the next moment. This may be one of the deepest forms of freedom the desert offers us. We are not trapped by the person we were five minutes ago. The pattern may be strong. The desire may still be present. Our emotions may not have changed at all.

Yet something new can begin before any of those things change.

We can choose differently. We can become honest. We can return to prayer. We can stop speaking. We can forgive. We can ask forgiveness. We can take one small step in another direction.

Silvanus calls this laying a foundation because transformation still requires building. One good moment is not the completed house. We will need another stone, and another, and another.

But every house begins somewhere. And if the structure falls, we begin again.

Day 9 told us to rise whenever we fall. Silvanus brings the teaching even closer. We do not need to wait until tomorrow to stand. There is a foundation beneath our feet right now.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *This moment is a beginning.*

**Breathing out:** *Help me build from here.*
