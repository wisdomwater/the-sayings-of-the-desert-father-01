## Day 70 — Do Not Force the Word

### Abba Macarius the Great

Abba Macarius once returned from visiting Abba Anthony, and the other fathers gathered around him. During their conversation, Macarius mentioned something he had asked Anthony, but the brothers quickly moved on to other subjects. They did not ask what Anthony had said in reply. Macarius therefore let the matter rest and did not force the teaching upon them.

One of the fathers later explained that an elder may sometimes begin speaking about something that could help others. But if no one shows interest or asks further, the elder should not continue simply for the sake of being heard. Wisdom needs a receptive listener as much as it needs someone willing to speak. Otherwise even a good word can become unnecessary speech.

### Devotional

There is a generosity in wanting to share something meaningful. We discover an insight, learn a lesson, or hear something that seems important, and naturally want others to receive it too. Macarius reminds us that even truth can be offered in the wrong way.

A useful word cannot always be pushed into another person. Readiness matters because people hear differently depending on what they are seeking, suffering, or prepared to receive. What awakens one person may simply pass another by.

This requires restraint from the one who speaks. We may feel tempted to explain more, repeat ourselves, or make the point stronger when someone does not respond. Macarius shows another possibility: we can allow the silence to remain.

That silence is not indifference. He had something he believed might help the brothers, and he offered enough for them to pursue it. When they did not, he respected their freedom instead of turning wisdom into pressure.

The same principle applies far beyond spiritual teaching. Advice given before someone wants it can feel like judgment, while the same words offered later may become a gift. Love pays attention not only to what is true, but also to whether the moment is ready for truth to be spoken.

There is humility here for teachers, parents, friends, and anyone who enjoys explaining things. We are not responsible for making another person understand everything we see. Sometimes our task is simply to make the door visible and let them decide whether to enter.

This also teaches us something about listening. The brothers lost an opportunity because they did not recognize that Macarius was quietly opening a door. Receptivity requires curiosity, attention, and the willingness to ask another question when something seems worth pursuing.

Wisdom therefore moves in both directions. The speaker learns not to force the word, and the listener learns not to let every meaningful opening pass unnoticed. When both are present, conversation becomes less about delivering answers and more about receiving what the moment is ready to hold.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Teach me when to speak.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me when to be silent.*
