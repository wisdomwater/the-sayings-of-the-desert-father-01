## Day 7 — Stop It Before It Grows

### Abba Poemen

A brother once came to Abba Poemen with a question from Scripture.

“What does it mean,” he asked, “not to repay evil with evil?”

Poemen told him that evil does not usually arrive fully formed. It moves through us in stages.

First it enters the heart.

Then it begins to show itself in the way we look at another person.

If it is allowed to continue, it finds its way into our words.

And finally, what began as an inner movement becomes something we do.

So Poemen told the brother: if you can cleanse the heart, begin there. If the feeling has already reached your face, guard your tongue. If it has already become speech, stop it quickly before it grows into an act of retaliation.

Do not despair because the movement has begun.

Interrupt it wherever you become aware.

### Devotional

Poemen understands something remarkably subtle about human behavior: our actions usually have a history.

The cruel word did not begin when it left our mouth. The retaliatory email did not begin when we pressed send. The coldness we show someone may have been forming quietly for hours, days, or even years before it became visible.

Something happened first in the heart.

Perhaps we felt wounded. Perhaps someone embarrassed us, ignored us, disappointed us, or failed to become who we wanted them to be. The original hurt may even have been entirely real.

Then the mind begins its work.

We replay the conversation. We imagine what we should have said. We gather evidence. We assign motives. Before long, the person who hurt us is no longer simply a complicated human being who acted badly in one moment. They become a character in a story we are constructing.

Poemen watches this progression with extraordinary care.

Heart.

Look.

Tongue.

Action.

At each stage, there is still an opening.

We often imagine self-control as something that happens at the final moment: *Don't yell. Don't retaliate. Don't send the message.* But by then the inner movement may have gathered considerable momentum. Poemen invites us to notice much earlier, perhaps when resentment is still only a tightening in the chest or a slight change in the way we look at someone.

This is not an invitation to suppress emotion. Hurt needs to be acknowledged. Anger can reveal that something valuable has been violated. But there is a difference between allowing an emotion to tell us something and allowing it to decide what happens next.

The earlier we notice, the more freedom we have.

Yet Poemen also seems wonderfully realistic. If we fail to catch it in the heart, the opportunity is not lost. Catch it in the eyes. If not there, catch it in the tongue. If words have already escaped, stop before words become actions.

There is no point in saying, “Well, I have already failed.”

The next boundary still matters.

Perhaps this is one of the hidden meanings of repentance. We do not have to wait until an entire destructive pattern has played itself out before turning around. Repentance can happen halfway through a sentence. It can happen while the angry message is still sitting unsent. It can happen in the moment we notice that we are enjoying our resentment a little too much.

We can stop.

We can return.

And with practice, perhaps we begin noticing the movement earlier and earlier, until what once controlled our actions becomes something we can simply observe passing through the heart.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Help me notice what is rising.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me where to stop.*
