## Day 3 — Begin Again

### Abba Arsenius

Abba Arsenius was once troubled by his thoughts and prayed for guidance.

A voice came to him:

“Arsenius, flee. Be silent. Be still.”

These were not accomplishments to collect, but a way of life to return to. Again and again, Arsenius was called away from the noise that scattered him and back toward the quiet place where he could attend to God.

### Devotional

There is a subtle temptation in any spiritual practice to begin measuring ourselves by what we have already done. We remember the prayers we have prayed, the books we have read, the habits we have established, the insights we have gathered. Without quite noticing it, yesterday's faithfulness can become today's identity.

But yesterday cannot pray today's prayer.

Arsenius had already left an extraordinary life behind. He had lived among the powerful and educated of the imperial world before withdrawing into the desert. If anyone could have looked backward and constructed a story about how far he had come, it was Arsenius. Yet the wisdom associated with him continually draws him back to something much simpler: flee what scatters you, become quiet, return to stillness.

The invitation is not to accumulate a spiritual résumé. It is to begin again.

This can feel strangely freeing. We do not have to recreate yesterday's experience of God, nor do we have to condemn ourselves because today's prayer feels distracted when yesterday's felt peaceful. We do not need to prove that we are progressing. We only need to become available to the moment we have actually been given.

Perhaps this is one reason contemplative practice can feel both ancient and new every morning. We may sit in the same chair, breathe the same breath prayer, light the same candle, or enter the same silence, but we have never inhabited this particular moment before. Today's fears are here. Today's desires are here. Today's weariness and gratitude are here. And God, if God is to be encountered anywhere, must be encountered here.

Beginning again also loosens our grip on failure. If we believe the spiritual life is a steadily rising line, every bad day becomes evidence that we have lost ground. But the desert offers a different image. The path is made of returns. We wander, notice, and return; become distracted, notice, and return; become entangled in ourselves, notice, and return.

Even our wandering can teach us where home is.

Yesterday may contain wisdom worth remembering, but it cannot substitute for today's attention. Tomorrow may contain possibilities worth hoping for, but we cannot live there yet. What remains is this breath, this moment, this quiet invitation to turn again toward what is deepest and most real.

The spiritual life begins here.

And then, tomorrow, it begins here again.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *I begin again.*

**Breathing out:** *I return to you.*
