## Day 3 — Make a Beginning

### Abba Arsenius

One day Abba Arsenius was struggling fiercely in his cell. His thoughts pressed against him so heavily that those who cared for him could hear his distress from outside, and they stopped near the door to listen. They heard a man who was not trying to hide the struggle from God.

Arsenius did not remind God of the years he had spent in the desert, the comforts he had surrendered, or the prayers he had already prayed. He did not reach backward for evidence that he had earned some kind of spiritual standing. Instead, he cried out to God and asked not to be abandoned.

He admitted that he had nothing good to place before God as proof of his worthiness. Yet he trusted that God's goodness was larger than his lack, and from that place he asked for one thing: the grace to begin. Even after all his years of devotion, Arsenius was willing to stand again at the starting place.

### Devotional

There is something startling about hearing these words from Arsenius. By the time this story was remembered, he was already regarded as one of the great elders of the desert, a man who had left behind privilege and status, embraced solitude, and devoted years to prayer. Yet in a moment of struggle, he does not say, “Look how far I have come.”

He asks to begin.

Perhaps one of the quiet dangers of spiritual practice is that we eventually begin keeping a ledger. We remember the years we have prayed, the books we have read, the disciplines we have maintained, the wounds we have worked through, and the ways we believe we have changed. None of these things are meaningless, but they can slowly become evidence we present to ourselves that we ought to be farther along than we are.

Then an old fear returns, or an old resentment appears. A familiar craving wakes up, and we find ourselves thinking something we believed we had already outgrown. Suddenly the whole history of our practice can seem called into question.

Arsenius responds differently. He does not use yesterday's faithfulness to argue with today's struggle, and he does not turn today's struggle into proof that yesterday was wasted. He simply stands before God without a résumé and asks for the grace to begin from where he actually is.

There is great mercy in this. Beginning does not require us to erase everything that came before, nor does it mean that our past growth was unreal. It simply means that the past no longer has to determine what this moment is allowed to become.

We do not have to carry yesterday's success as a burden. We do not have to prove that we are still the person we thought we had become, and we do not have to carry yesterday's failure as a sentence either. The present moment gives us another place from which to turn.

This may be one of the deepest movements of the contemplative life. We sit down to pray and discover that the mind is scattered, so we begin. We notice that we have been living from fear again, or that desire has pulled us off center, or that impatience has shaped the way we treated someone, and we begin again from there.

The spiritual path may contain fewer permanent arrivals than we imagine. Again and again we discover ourselves at some new edge of our own incompleteness, facing something we thought we had already resolved. What matters is not whether we return to the starting place, but whether we recognize that the starting place remains open to us.

Arsenius does not seem embarrassed to find himself there. Perhaps after enough years he had discovered that the beginning was never very far away, because every encounter with God asks for some fresh willingness to receive what is here now. We cannot pray yesterday's prayer or make yesterday's choice again.

And perhaps neither is God very far away.

We do not need a perfect record in order to take the next faithful step. We need only enough honesty to stand where we are, enough humility to stop pretending we are somewhere else, and enough hope to believe that another beginning is still possible.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Let me begin again.*

**Breathing out:** *Meet me here, O God.*
