## Day 3 — Make a Beginning

### Abba Arsenius

One day Abba Arsenius was struggling fiercely in his cell. His thoughts pressed against him so heavily that those who cared for him could hear his distress from outside.

They stopped near the door and listened.

Arsenius was not defending himself before God. He was not reminding God of the years he had spent in the desert, the comforts he had surrendered, or the prayers he had already prayed.

Instead, they heard him cry out:

“O God, do not leave me. I have nothing good to place before you. But because you are good, let me begin.”

### Devotional

There is something startling about hearing these words from Arsenius. By the time this story was remembered, he was already regarded as one of the great elders of the desert. He had left behind privilege and status, embraced solitude, given himself to prayer, and spent years pursuing the life he believed God had called him to live.

And yet, in a moment of struggle, he does not say, “Look how far I have come.”

He says, in effect, “Let me begin.”

Perhaps one of the quiet dangers of spiritual practice is that we eventually begin keeping a ledger. We remember the years we have prayed, the books we have read, the disciplines we have maintained, the wounds we have worked through, and the ways we believe we have changed. None of these things are meaningless, but they can slowly become evidence we present to ourselves that we ought to be farther along than we are.

Then an old fear returns. An old resentment appears. A familiar craving wakes up. We find ourselves thinking something we believed we had outgrown, and suddenly the whole history of our practice seems called into question.

Arsenius responds differently.

He does not use yesterday's faithfulness to argue with today's struggle. Nor does he turn today's struggle into proof that yesterday was wasted. He simply stands before God without a résumé and asks for the grace to begin from where he actually is.

There is great mercy in this.

Beginning does not require us to erase everything that came before. It means that the past no longer has to determine what this moment is allowed to become. We do not have to carry yesterday's success as a burden, and we do not have to carry yesterday's failure as a sentence.

We can begin here.

This may be one of the deepest movements of the contemplative life. We sit down to pray and discover that the mind is scattered. Begin. We notice that we have been living from fear again. Begin. We speak impatiently, become caught in desire, lose our center, or forget what we thought we had learned.

Begin.

Arsenius does not seem embarrassed to return to the starting place. Perhaps after enough years, he had discovered that the starting place was never very far away.

And perhaps neither is God.

We do not need a perfect record in order to take the next faithful step. We need only enough honesty to stand where we are and enough hope to believe that another beginning is still possible.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Let me begin again.*

**Breathing out:** *Meet me here, O God.*
