## Day 77 — The Strength to Remain Silent

### Abba Poemen

Abba Poemen reflected on the saying of Jesus that by our words we will be justified and by our words we will be condemned. He believed that if a person truly remembered this, they would become much more careful about speaking. Silence would begin to look less like emptiness and more like restraint. Not every thought, reaction, or injury would need to become words.

Poemen’s counsel is especially demanding when circumstances press against us. It is easy to remain quiet when nothing has disturbed us. The real test comes when we feel misunderstood, criticized, contradicted, or provoked. Then silence can become a form of endurance rather than mere absence of speech.

### Devotional

Words often arrive fastest when we are under pressure. We want to explain ourselves, defend our intentions, correct another person, or make sure our version of events is understood. The urgency can make speaking feel almost irresistible.

Poemen asks us to remember that speech has consequences. Once words leave us, they enter another person’s mind and heart where we no longer control what they do. A moment of anger can therefore continue long after the feeling that produced it has passed.

Silence creates a small space between feeling and action. In that space we may discover that the first thing we wanted to say was not the thing we most deeply meant. Restraint gives the heart time to catch up with the tongue.

This does not mean never speaking difficult truth. There are times when honesty, correction, boundaries, or protest are necessary. Poemen’s concern is with speech that escapes us before discernment has had time to work.

Adversity makes this practice particularly revealing. When we are contradicted or wounded, we quickly learn how much approval, control, or vindication matters to us. The pressure exposes what calm circumstances allowed to remain hidden.

That discovery need not become another reason for shame. We can simply notice the surge within us and allow it to become part of prayer. Silence can hold the reaction long enough for us to understand what it is asking for.

Sometimes we will still decide that something must be said. But words that have passed through silence tend to carry a different weight than words spoken from immediate reaction. They can become clearer, gentler, and more truthful.

Poemen invites us to treat speech as something precious enough not to spend carelessly. Endurance sometimes means bearing the discomfort of not answering immediately. In that pause, we may find that what seemed urgent no longer needs to be said at all.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Quiet what rises too quickly.*

**Breathing out:** *Let my words come from peace.*
