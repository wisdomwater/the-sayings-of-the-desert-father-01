## Day 81 — When Sanity Looks Strange

### Abba Anthony

Abba Anthony said that a time would come when people would lose their bearings. When they encountered someone who had not joined them, they would turn against that person and call him mad. Their accusation would not arise because he had lost his mind, but because he no longer resembled them. Difference itself would become evidence of disorder.

Anthony’s saying is severe, but its point is simple. A crowd can normalize patterns that are unhealthy, cruel, anxious, or spiritually destructive. When that happens, refusing the pattern can make a person appear unreasonable. Freedom may require accepting that others will not always understand why you are choosing differently.

### Devotional

Most of us want to belong. We learn what is normal by watching the people around us, and we absorb expectations long before we consciously evaluate them. That makes the judgment of the group unusually powerful.

Anthony reminds us that normality and health are not always the same thing. A behavior can be common without being wise, and a desire can be widely shared without being life-giving. Discernment sometimes begins when we stop asking whether everyone else is doing it.

This can be uncomfortable because communities reinforce themselves. When one person steps outside a familiar pattern, others may experience that choice as criticism even when none was intended. Your refusal can expose a question everyone else preferred not to ask.

The temptation then is to return to the group simply to relieve the discomfort. We may silence a conviction, resume an old habit, or pretend agreement because being thought strange feels costly. Anthony suggests that freedom sometimes includes the willingness to bear that misunderstanding.

At the same time, this saying should not become permission for self-righteousness. Being different does not automatically make us wise, and disagreement with the majority is not proof that we alone see clearly. Humility requires us to remain open to correction even while resisting pressure to conform.

The deeper task is to become less governed by approval. We listen carefully, examine ourselves honestly, and then act according to what appears true and good. The goal is not rebellion but integrity.

There may be seasons when faithfulness feels lonely. A simpler life can look foolish in a culture of excess, forgiveness can look weak in a culture of retaliation, and stillness can look unproductive in a culture of constant activity. The values around us inevitably shape what appears sensible.

Anthony invites us to notice which standards we have accepted without examination. Some forms of freedom begin by allowing ourselves to look strange. We do not need to reject the world around us, but neither do we have to become whatever the world around us expects.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Free me from the need to fit in.*

**Breathing out:** *Root me in what is true.*
