## Day 80 — Before the Fire Burns Clear

### Amma Syncletica

Amma Syncletica said that those who begin drawing near to God often encounter struggle and difficulty before they experience joy. She compared the spiritual life to trying to kindle a fire. At first the smoke fills the eyes and brings tears while the flame struggles to catch. Only afterward does the fire begin to burn clearly and give what the person was seeking.

For Syncletica, the early difficulty is not necessarily evidence that something has gone wrong. The smoke can belong to the same process that eventually produces the flame. She therefore encourages perseverance through the uncomfortable beginning rather than expecting immediate spiritual consolation. Tears and effort may sometimes accompany the slow kindling of a deeper life.

### Devotional

Beginnings often carry expectations that reality quickly disappoints. We decide to pray more faithfully, change an old pattern, forgive someone, or become more attentive to God, and we expect the decision itself to bring peace. Instead, the first thing we encounter may be smoke.

Old habits resist being disturbed. Distraction becomes louder, desire becomes more noticeable, and the parts of ourselves we hoped to leave behind seem suddenly more active. The discomfort can make us wonder whether the practice is helping at all.

Syncletica offers another interpretation. Perhaps the difficulty is not always proof that the fire has failed to catch, but evidence that something is actually being disturbed. Change can make hidden resistance visible before anything new feels stable.

This does not mean suffering should be sought for its own sake. Smoke is not the goal of lighting a fire, and tears are not the goal of spiritual practice. The image only asks us not to confuse an uncomfortable beginning with a meaningless one.

There are many forms of formation that work this way. Learning to sit in silence may initially expose restlessness, while learning restraint may make craving feel stronger because we are finally noticing it. Practicing forgiveness may first bring us into fuller contact with the hurt we had been carrying.

The temptation is to stop precisely when the difficulty becomes visible. We may conclude that prayer does not work, that we lack discipline, or that transformation belongs to people with stronger wills. Syncletica asks us to stay near the small flame a little longer.

Eventually, some struggles do change character. What once required constant effort can become more natural, and what once felt like deprivation can begin to reveal another kind of freedom. The joy Syncletica describes is not manufactured by forcing ourselves to feel joyful but discovered through sustained faithfulness.

We do not control when the smoke will clear. Our task is smaller: to keep tending what seems worthy of becoming a fire. When our eyes sting and the flame remains small, we can remember that beginnings are often less beautiful than what they are beginning to make possible.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Keep the flame alive in me.*

**Breathing out:** *Carry me through the smoke.*
