## Day 32 — Begin Where You Are

### Abba Anthony

Some brothers came to Abba Anthony and asked him for a word about how they might be saved. Anthony first pointed them back to Scripture, but they pressed him to speak more directly. So he reminded them of Jesus’ teaching about turning the other cheek.

The brothers admitted that they could not do it. Anthony lowered the demand and suggested that they at least endure the first blow without retaliation, but they said they could not do that either. He then told them not to return evil for evil, and once again they confessed that they were unable.

At last Anthony turned to his disciple and asked him to prepare some food for them, treating them almost as though they were sick. He said that if they could do none of the things he had offered, there was little more he could prescribe. What they needed now was prayer.

### Devotional

There is something almost humorous in the exchange. Anthony keeps making the path easier, and the brothers keep admitting that even the easier version is beyond them. They do not pretend to be stronger than they are.

Anthony does not respond by shaming them. He does not accuse them of laziness or question whether they are serious about God. Instead, he reaches a point where instruction gives way to compassion. If they cannot yet do what is being asked, then perhaps the next faithful act is simply to acknowledge that weakness before God.

This is an important form of mercy. We often know what another person should do. From the outside, the next step can seem obvious: forgive, stop reacting, let it go, set the boundary, change the habit, tell the truth, be patient. The prescription may even be correct.

But correctness is not the same as capacity. A person may understand exactly what would be healthier and still be unable to do it today. Fear may be too strong, the wound too fresh, the habit too deeply rooted, or the nervous system too overwhelmed. Repeating the instruction more forcefully does not always create the strength to obey it.

Anthony seems to recognize this. He begins with the ideal and then keeps moving toward the actual person standing before him. The goal does not disappear, but mercy changes the way he approaches the distance between the two. He refuses to confuse honesty about weakness with rejection.

We can learn to do the same with ourselves. Sometimes we know perfectly well what we ought to do and are frustrated that knowing has not produced change. We tell ourselves the same instruction again, only louder, and then add shame when we fail to follow it. Eventually the inner voice becomes less like guidance and more like accusation.

Anthony offers another response. If you cannot yet do the whole thing, tell the truth about that. If even the smaller step feels impossible, tell the truth about that too. The admission itself may become the beginning of prayer.

Prayer, in this sense, is not a substitute for effort. It is what remains when effort has reached the edge of its present strength. We stop pretending that willpower alone can carry us and begin asking for help.

There is humility in that. The brothers do not leave Anthony with a new technique to master. They leave with their weakness exposed and with the recognition that they need grace. That may be more spiritually fruitful than performing strength they do not possess.

Mercy toward others often begins in the same place. We can hold a vision of what healing might look like without demanding that another person arrive there on our schedule. Sometimes the most loving thing we can do is remain beside them while the capacity for the next step slowly grows.

This does not mean abandoning truth. Anthony never says that retaliation is good or that the Gospel can simply be ignored. He simply distinguishes the truth of the path from the pace at which a particular person can presently walk it.

Perhaps judgment asks, *Why are you not there yet?*

Mercy asks, *Where are you now, and what can be done from here?*

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Meet me where I am.*

**Breathing out:** *Give me strength for the next step.*
