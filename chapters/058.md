## Day 58 — Rest in Every Place

### Abba Poemen

Abba Poemen said that if a person gives himself to silence, he will have rest wherever he lives. He does not promise that the surroundings will become quiet or that difficult people will disappear. The rest he describes begins within the person rather than in the circumstances around them.

For Poemen, silence is more than simply refusing to speak. It is a way of no longer feeding every thought, irritation, opinion, and impulse with words. When the inner need to react begins to settle, peace becomes less dependent on finding the perfect place.

### Devotional

We often imagine that peace is waiting somewhere else. If the environment were quieter, the schedule lighter, the people easier, or the demands fewer, then perhaps we could finally settle. Poemen reverses the direction of the search.

He does not tell us to find a peaceful place. He tells us to become capable of silence. A quiet environment may help, but no external arrangement can guarantee inner rest. We carry our own noise with us.

Much of that noise is created by commentary. Something happens, and almost immediately the mind begins explaining, judging, defending, rehearsing, predicting, or preparing a response. The event may last only a few seconds while the internal conversation continues for hours. Silence begins when we discover that not every thought requires another thought.

This is not the same as suppressing emotion. Anger, grief, fear, and desire can all be honestly felt without being endlessly narrated. Sometimes the story we keep telling about the feeling gives it more energy than the feeling itself. Silence allows the experience to be present without constantly adding fuel.

Speech can work the same way. A small frustration can become larger as we repeat it, explain it to several people, and refine the case for why we were right. Each retelling may strengthen the emotional groove. Choosing silence at the right moment can interrupt that process.

Poemen is not recommending withdrawal from every conversation. There are words that need to be spoken, truths that require courage, apologies that must be made, and relationships that depend on honest communication. Silence becomes harmful when it is used to punish, avoid, or conceal what love requires us to address. The silence Poemen describes is meant to create freedom rather than distance.

That freedom becomes visible when circumstances are unpleasant. If our peace depends entirely on others behaving properly, we will spend much of life trying to manage them. If it depends on every plan succeeding, disappointment will continually carry us away. Inner silence gives us somewhere to stand when events refuse to cooperate.

The practice may begin with very small pauses. Before responding to an irritating comment, we can allow one breath to pass. Before sending the message, explaining ourselves, or joining the argument already happening in our head, we can notice whether anything truly needs to be said. Sometimes the pause changes the words, and sometimes it makes the words unnecessary.

Silence also creates room to hear what noise can hide. Beneath our first reaction there may be fear, sadness, embarrassment, longing, or a need for reassurance. Beneath even those movements there may be a deeper quiet that does not need to solve everything immediately. Prayer often begins when we stop filling every empty space.

Poemen’s promise is surprisingly expansive. He does not say silence will give rest only in the desert, the monastery, or the ideal circumstances. He says rest can be found wherever we live. The location becomes less decisive as the heart learns not to chase every disturbance.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Quiet the noise within me.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to rest where I am.*
