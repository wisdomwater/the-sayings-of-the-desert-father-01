## Day 67 — A Rule for the Ordinary Day

### Abba Poemen

A brother asked Abba Poemen how he should live in his cell. Poemen gave him a simple pattern: work with his hands, eat with restraint, keep silence, meditate, and remain faithful to prayer. He also told him to watch his inner life carefully and not neglect the hidden movements of the heart. The rule was ordinary, repetitive, and meant to shape the whole day rather than produce a single dramatic experience.

Poemen added that when manual work stopped, the brother should turn naturally toward prayer. He also urged him to seek good companionship and avoid relationships that pulled him away from the life he was trying to cultivate. The spiritual life, in Poemen’s vision, is built from rhythms that support one another. Work, prayer, restraint, attention, and community become parts of one coherent way of living.

### Devotional

There is something reassuring about how ordinary Poemen’s counsel is. He does not give the brother a secret technique or an extraordinary spiritual achievement to pursue. He gives him a rhythm for the day.

Work has a place in that rhythm. The hands need something honest and useful to do, because idleness can leave the mind vulnerable to endless wandering. Ordinary labor can become a form of grounding when it is done with attention rather than hurry.

Restraint has a place as well. Poemen does not demand dramatic deprivation, but he does assume that appetite should not govern the whole day. Saying enough can sometimes create the space in which deeper desires become visible.

Silence and meditation give the mind somewhere to settle. Without pauses, the day can become a continuous stream of stimulus, reaction, and unfinished thought. Quiet allows us to notice what has been moving beneath the surface.

Prayer then becomes woven into the rhythm rather than added as an isolated religious task. When work stops, attention can turn toward God. The boundary between ordinary life and prayer begins to soften.

Poemen also includes self-watchfulness. External routines are not enough if the inner life remains unattended. We can work, fast, pray, and still be carried by resentment, pride, fear, or distraction without noticing.

His final concern is companionship. The people around us shape what begins to feel normal, desirable, and possible. Good company does not require perfect people, but it does mean relationships that help us become more awake and faithful.

A rule of life is useful because it reduces the need to reinvent the spiritual life every morning. We return to a few simple practices and let them carry us through changing moods and circumstances. Over time, ordinary rhythms can become a quiet architecture for attention.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Order my day around what gives life.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to return to what matters.*
