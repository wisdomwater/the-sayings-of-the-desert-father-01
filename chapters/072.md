## Day 72 — The Work of Winter

### Amma Theodora

Amma Theodora urged her listeners to keep entering by the narrow gate. She compared human life to trees that must stand through winter before they can bear fruit. The storms are not the fruit, but they are part of what prepares the tree to produce it. In the same way, trials and temptations can become part of our formation.

Theodora does not romanticize hardship for its own sake. Her image assumes that winter is difficult precisely because it strips away ease and exposes the tree to harsh conditions. Yet the season is not empty or meaningless. Something is being strengthened before the fruit becomes visible.

### Devotional

We often recognize growth only after it becomes visible. We notice the fruit, but not the long season of roots deepening beneath cold ground. Theodora asks us to take winter seriously.

Some seasons feel spiritually unproductive because nothing seems to be happening. Prayer feels dry, circumstances remain difficult, and the qualities we hoped to develop seem no closer than before. Yet hidden formation may still be taking place.

Winter does not create fruit by force. It changes the conditions in which the tree grows, requiring it to endure what summer never asks of it. Hardship can expose capacities that comfort leaves untouched.

This does not mean suffering automatically makes us wiser. Pain can also harden, exhaust, or wound us, and some storms leave damage that needs healing. Theodora’s point is that adversity can become formative when we meet it with patience, attention, and faithfulness.

Trials also reveal what supports us. A tree survives winter because of roots developed long before the storm arrived. In the same way, ordinary practices of prayer, friendship, rest, truthfulness, and humility often become most precious when life becomes difficult.

There may be little visible reward during such a season. We may simply keep showing up, keep praying, keep asking for help, and keep choosing not to abandon what matters. That quiet endurance is itself part of the work.

The image of winter also reminds us that seasons change. A tree is not failing because it is bare in January. There are times when fruitfulness looks like growth, and times when faithfulness looks like surviving the cold.

Theodora invites us to trust a longer process than immediate results can reveal. What feels barren may still be preparing something we cannot yet see. The storm is not the destination, but it may become part of the way we learn to bear fruit.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Root me deeply in the storm.*

**Breathing out:** *Prepare in me the fruit to come.*
