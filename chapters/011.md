# Day 11 — Return to the Water

### Abba Anthony

Abba Anthony said:

A fish cannot remain out of water for long and still live.

In the same way, a monk who continually wanders away from his cell and fills his time with noise and company gradually loses the inner stillness he has been cultivating.

So Anthony told his brothers to return quickly to their cells, just as a fish returns to the sea.

The cell was not merely a room.

It was the place where they remembered how to pay attention.

### Devotional

Anthony's image is severe, but also wonderfully simple. A fish does not return to water because water is an interesting spiritual discipline. It returns because water is the environment in which it can live. Out of water, the fish may survive for a little while, but something essential is being lost with every passing moment.

Anthony believed stillness could become something like that for the human soul. His monks had literal cells in the Egyptian desert, small places of prayer and work to which they continually returned. Most of us do not. We live among families, workplaces, traffic, phones, notifications, conversations, responsibilities, and an almost endless stream of information.

But perhaps the deeper meaning of the cell remains. There are places, practices, and moments in which we become able to hear ourselves again. We may discover it sitting quietly before anyone else wakes. We may find it while walking without headphones, praying in a familiar chair, tending a garden, driving without filling the silence, or simply putting the phone down for ten minutes and allowing nothing new to enter.

The important thing is not escaping other people. Anthony's image can easily be misunderstood that way. The world is not poison, and relationship is not the enemy of contemplation. The problem is that constant outward movement can gradually make us strangers to our own interior life.

We become accustomed to stimulation. One conversation ends and another begins. One article leads to another. One notification becomes ten. Every empty space is quickly filled, until silence itself begins to feel uncomfortable. Then we may discover that we have been living outside the water for quite some time.

The cell is where we return. It may be an actual place, but eventually it becomes something inward: a willingness to stop following every thought, every urgency, every invitation to react. We withdraw for a moment from the endless demand to look outward and allow attention to settle again.

At first, nothing dramatic may happen. We simply notice that we are tired. Or restless. Or afraid. Or carrying an emotion we had been too busy to feel.

This is part of returning to the water too. Stillness does not immediately make us peaceful. Sometimes it first reveals how far from peace we have wandered.

Anthony does not tell the fish to feel guilty for leaving the sea. He simply tells it to return. Perhaps that is enough for us as well. We do not need to condemn ourselves for distraction. We only need to remember where life becomes deeper again.

And return.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Draw me back to stillness.*

**Breathing out:** *Let me rest in you.*
