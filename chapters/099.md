## Day 99 — The Wisdom of Our Mortality

### Amma Sarah

Amma Sarah once offered a brief teaching about the way she approached the spiritual life. She said that whenever she placed her foot upon a ladder, she brought the remembrance of death before her eyes before taking the next step. It was a simple image, drawn from an ordinary movement of daily life. Yet for Sarah, even the act of climbing could become an occasion to remember the fragile and temporary nature of human existence.

Her words reflected a practice common among the Desert Fathers and Mothers, who frequently contemplated their mortality as a way of cultivating spiritual attention. Sarah did not describe death as a distant event to be considered only in old age or illness. She brought its reality into the present, allowing it to shape the choices she made while life was still unfolding. The remembrance of death became a means of remembering how to live.&#x20;

### Devotional

We spend much of our lives behaving as though time were an unlimited resource. We make plans for the coming years, postpone difficult conversations, and assume that the opportunities available today will remain available tomorrow. Even when we acknowledge intellectually that our lives are finite, we rarely allow that knowledge to influence our ordinary decisions. Mortality remains a distant abstraction rather than a reality that shapes how we live.

Sarah invites us to bring that distant awareness into the present moment. Her image of placing a foot upon a ladder is striking because it connects the certainty of death with something so ordinary. She does not retreat into an elaborate meditation or wait for a moment of solemn reflection. She simply remembers, before taking another step, that her life is not without limits.

At first, such a practice may seem unnecessarily dark. We might wonder why anyone would choose to contemplate death when there is already so much uncertainty and suffering in the world. Yet remembering mortality need not mean becoming preoccupied with fear or imagining all the ways our lives might end. It can instead help us recognize the preciousness of the life we are living now.

Consider how differently we might approach an ordinary encounter if we remembered that our time together is finite. A conversation with a loved one might become less about winning an argument and more about understanding the person before us. We might become more willing to express gratitude, offer forgiveness, or simply listen without distraction. The awareness that an opportunity will not last forever can deepen our willingness to receive it while it remains.

The same awareness can illuminate the way we spend our attention. So much of our energy is consumed by concerns that seem urgent but may have little lasting significance. We worry about our reputation, compare our accomplishments with those of others, and rehearse frustrations that have already passed. Remembering that our lives are brief can loosen the hold of these concerns and help us distinguish what matters from what merely demands our attention.

There is also a kind of humility that grows from accepting our mortality. We are not permanent fixtures in the world, nor can we control the unfolding of every circumstance around us. Our projects will remain unfinished, our understanding will remain incomplete, and much of what we cherish will eventually pass beyond our grasp. Yet these limitations need not diminish the meaning of our lives. They may help us recognize that meaning does not depend upon possessing or preserving everything we love.

For those who understand life as held within the love of God, mortality also raises a deeper question about what it means to trust. We do not know precisely what lies beyond the boundaries of our present experience, and our hopes do not remove the mystery of death. Yet we may still learn to entrust our lives to the source from which they came. Perhaps faith is not the possession of certainty about what follows, but the willingness to live and love without requiring that every uncertainty be resolved.

Sarah's teaching invites us to approach each step with greater awareness of the gift we have received. We need not become solemn or fearful, nor must we continually remind ourselves that our time is running out. We can simply allow the knowledge of our mortality to awaken gratitude, tenderness, and a clearer sense of what deserves our love. When we remember that life is brief, we may discover a deeper freedom to inhabit it fully.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Let me remember the gift of this life.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to love while I am here.*
