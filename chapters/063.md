## Day 63 — Do the Work First

### Abba Theodore of Pherme

A brother came to Abba Theodore and began speaking about things he had never yet put into practice. Theodore compared him to someone who had not even found a ship or loaded the cargo, yet was already talking as though he had reached the city. The words had outrun the life beneath them. Theodore told him to do the work first, and then speak from what had actually been lived.

His warning is not against reflection, learning, or conversation. It is against mistaking understanding for transformation. A truth we can explain is not necessarily a truth we have embodied. Practice gives weight to words that ideas alone cannot provide.

### Devotional

It is easy to feel close to a truth because we can describe it well. We may understand patience, forgiveness, prayer, humility, or restraint at the level of concepts. The mind can arrive somewhere long before the rest of us does.

Theodore asks us to notice that gap without shame. There is nothing wrong with being at the beginning of the journey. The trouble comes when we begin speaking as though the journey has already been completed.

Modern life makes this especially easy. We can read, listen, discuss, post, teach, and collect insight faster than we can live any of it. Information accumulates quickly, while formation remains slow.

Theodore’s image of the ship restores sequence. First comes the boarding, then the loading, then the sailing, and only afterward the arrival. Spiritual life has its own sequence of repeated practice, failure, adjustment, and return.

This does not mean we must wait until we are perfect before speaking. None of the Desert Fathers had reached some final state from which they could talk without contradiction. Their authority came from having wrestled with what they taught.

There is a difference between saying, “This is true,” and saying, “I have learned to live this.” The first may come from study, while the second requires time. Humility allows us to know which sentence we are actually entitled to speak.

Theodore also points us back toward ordinary action. If we believe in forgiveness, there is someone to forgive. If we value prayer, there is a time to sit down and pray.

The same is true of any discipline we admire. We do not become patient by thinking beautifully about patience, nor generous by agreeing with generosity. The body eventually has to join the conviction.

There is freedom in letting practice come before performance. We no longer need to appear farther along than we are. We can simply take the next real step and allow our words to grow from there.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Teach me to live what I know.*

**Breathing out:** *Let my words follow the work.*
