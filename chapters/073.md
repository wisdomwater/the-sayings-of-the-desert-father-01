## Day 73 — When Stillness Becomes Difficult

### Amma Theodora

Amma Theodora taught that it is good to seek a life of peace and continual prayer. Yet she warned that the moment someone seriously turns toward stillness, resistance often appears. Discouragement, troubling thoughts, weakness, and bodily discomfort can make the person feel unable to continue. The desire for peace can uncover just how restless the heart and body have become.

She told of a monk who felt feverish and ill whenever he tried to pray. Instead of waiting until he felt better, he decided that if his life were ending, he wanted to pray while he still could. He rose and prayed despite how he felt. By refusing to let the resistance make the decision for him, he discovered that he could remain faithful.

### Devotional

We sometimes imagine that choosing stillness should immediately make us peaceful. Instead, silence can make us more aware of everything that was already restless within us. What felt manageable in distraction becomes much louder when there is nowhere else to look.

This can easily feel like failure. We sit down to pray and discover boredom, anxiety, physical discomfort, wandering thoughts, or the sudden desire to do almost anything else. Theodora tells us not to be surprised by this.

Stillness does not create all that resistance. Often it reveals what activity and noise had been helping us avoid. The difficulty can therefore become information rather than a verdict.

The ancient story speaks strongly about pushing through bodily resistance. We need not imitate that without discernment, especially when the body genuinely needs rest, treatment, or care. But there is wisdom in noticing the difference between incapacity and the discomfort of beginning.

Resistance often exaggerates itself. A few restless minutes can tell us that prayer is impossible, that silence is unbearable, or that we are simply not made for this kind of practice. Theodora encourages us to remain long enough to discover whether that first judgment is true.

Sometimes faithfulness means very little. We may sit for five minutes, breathe, offer a simple prayer, and notice how quickly the mind tries to escape. Staying for those five minutes can itself be the practice.

Over time, we become less frightened by resistance. We learn that distraction can be noticed without being obeyed, discouragement can be felt without becoming a command, and discomfort can be met with curiosity. Stillness becomes less about achieving a peaceful state and more about remaining present.

Theodora’s wisdom is not that every obstacle must be conquered by force. It is that resistance deserves to be examined before we let it decide our direction. Sometimes the difficulty is not a sign to stop, but the very place where attention begins to deepen.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Meet me in the resistance.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to remain.*
