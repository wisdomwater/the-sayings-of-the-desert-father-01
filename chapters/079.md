## Day 79 — When the Temptation Returns

### Abba Sisoes

Abba Joseph asked Abba Sisoes how long a person must keep cutting off the passions. Sisoes responded by asking whether Joseph really wanted to know how long this work would continue. When Joseph said yes, Sisoes told him to cut off the passion whenever it appeared. The answer offered no promise that temptation would soon disappear.

Sisoes places the emphasis not on eliminating every future struggle, but on responding faithfully to the struggle that is present now. A passion may return even after it has been resisted many times. Its return does not erase the choices that came before it. Each new appearance simply creates another moment in which freedom can be practiced.

### Devotional

We often assume that spiritual progress should make temptation disappear. If the same desire, fear, resentment, or habit returns, we may conclude that nothing has changed. Sisoes gives us a different way to measure growth.

The question is not only whether the temptation appears. The deeper question is what we do when it appears. Something can still knock at the door without being given permission to enter.

This matters because repeated temptation can become discouraging. We may pray sincerely, resist faithfully, and still find the same pattern returning days or even hours later. It is easy then to interpret recurrence as failure.

But recurrence and surrender are not the same thing. A thought can arise without becoming an action, and a desire can be felt without becoming a command. The space between impulse and response is where practice continues.

Prayer works within that space as well. We sometimes imagine that successful prayer should remove the struggle altogether. Yet prayer may instead help us remain awake, choose again, and keep returning when the struggle remains.

This makes spiritual growth less dramatic but perhaps more durable. Freedom may develop through hundreds of small decisions rather than one decisive victory. Each time we notice the passion and choose differently, another small path is being worn into the heart.

There is also compassion in Sisoes’s answer. He does not tell Joseph that a mature person will eventually stop being tempted. He assumes that the work may continue and gives him something simple to do when it does.

So when an old temptation returns, we need not begin with despair. We can notice it, bring it into prayer, and choose what leads toward life again. Yesterday’s struggle does not determine today’s response.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Meet me when the struggle returns.*

**Breathing out:** *Give me freedom to choose again.*
