## Day 68 — As Though I Had Just Begun

### Abba Pambo

As Abba Pambo was dying, holy men gathered around him. He looked back over his years in the desert and remembered that he had lived from the work of his own hands. He also said that he could not remember regretting the words he had spoken. Yet at the end of such a disciplined life, he still felt as though he had only begun to serve God.

Pambo does not erase the faithfulness of his life, but neither does he turn it into a spiritual résumé. Decades of labor have not made him feel finished or superior. The closer he comes to the end, the less interested he seems in measuring how far he has advanced. His life remains open before God even at the threshold of death.

### Devotional

There is something striking about Pambo’s refusal to declare himself accomplished. By ordinary standards, his life could easily have justified a sense of spiritual success. Instead, he arrives at the end with the posture of a beginner.

This is very different from saying that all his effort was worthless. Pambo clearly remembers the work, discipline, and restraint that shaped his life. He simply refuses to turn those things into proof that he has mastered the spiritual life.

A beginner remains teachable. There is still more to discover, more to receive, and more room for transformation. Pambo carries that openness with him all the way to the end.

This protects spiritual practice from becoming another system of measurement. Years of prayer, service, study, or discipline can quietly become numbers we use to reassure ourselves that we are progressing. Pambo releases the need to know exactly where he ranks.

His humility also keeps yesterday’s faithfulness from replacing today’s response. A long history of good choices does not eliminate the need to choose again now. Every day still places something new before us.

There is freedom in beginning again this way. We do not have to defend an image of ourselves as advanced, consistent, or especially wise. We can simply return to the work with the openness of someone who is still learning.

Pambo’s deathbed words also widen the horizon of spiritual growth. If a whole lifetime can still feel like the beginning, then perhaps there is no point at which the mystery of God becomes exhausted. Growth is not a staircase with a final platform we eventually reach.

The invitation is therefore not to finish the spiritual life, but to remain available to it. We work faithfully, speak carefully, love as well as we can, and keep beginning. Even after many years, the next step can still be taken with the humility of a beginner.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Keep me open to beginning again.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to serve without measuring.*
