## Day 37 — Keep Your Conscience Clear

### Abba Joseph of Panephysis

A brother came to Abba Joseph and admitted that he did not have the strength for severe hardship. He also said that he was unable to work enough to give generously to others. Joseph did not rebuke him for lacking the capacities he wished he had.

Instead, Joseph gave him something smaller and closer at hand. If he could do none of those greater things, he should at least keep his conscience free of evil toward his neighbor. Joseph told him that this alone could become a path of salvation.

### Devotional

We often imagine the spiritual life through the things we wish we were capable of doing. We picture deeper prayer, greater generosity, stronger discipline, more patience, more courage, and perhaps a life less tangled by weakness. The distance between that imagined self and the person we actually are can become discouraging.

Joseph begins with the person who is actually there.

The brother does not present himself as especially impressive. He cannot endure the kind of hardship he associates with holiness, and he cannot even perform the charitable work he thinks he should be doing. He comes to Joseph with empty hands.

Joseph does not tell him to try harder.

Instead, he brings the spiritual life into the ordinary territory of relationship. If you cannot accomplish the large thing, begin by refusing evil toward the person beside you. Guard what happens in the heart when another human being enters your thoughts.

That may sound easier than it is.

We can refrain from outward harm while privately rehearsing resentment, contempt, jealousy, or accusation. We may smile while constructing arguments against someone, behave politely while secretly enjoying their failure, or tell ourselves that our hostility is justified because we are technically correct. The conscience knows the difference.

Joseph turns our attention there.

The question is not only whether we have harmed someone visibly. It is whether we are allowing their image to become a place where bitterness lives. What happens inside us when we remember their name?

This is a demanding kind of honesty.

We may discover that we have not shouted, retaliated, or acted cruelly, but we have been carrying an inner version of the person that exists mainly to be condemned. We replay what they did, assign motives, imagine confrontations, and strengthen the case against them. Even without speaking, the relationship becomes poisoned within us.

Keeping the conscience clear does not mean manufacturing warm feelings.

Some wounds require distance, some relationships need boundaries, and some actions need to be named plainly as harmful. Joseph does not ask us to deny reality. He asks us not to cultivate evil in response to it.

That distinction is important.

We can protect ourselves without wishing another person harm. We can disagree without contempt, confront without humiliation, and step away without needing the other person to suffer. The conscience remains clear when truth is joined to love.

There is something merciful in Joseph giving this teaching to someone who feels spiritually incapable.

He does not demand spectacular holiness. He gives the brother a practice that can happen in the next conversation, the next memory, the next irritation, and the next moment when judgment begins rising. Holiness becomes immediate.

Perhaps this is where many of us can begin.

We may not be able to fix every weakness or perform some heroic act of devotion. But we can notice when resentment is becoming pleasurable, when another person's failure makes us feel superior, or when we are about to turn pain into retaliation. In that moment, there is still a choice.

Joseph places tremendous weight on that quiet choice.

A clear conscience toward our neighbor may look small compared with fasting, sacrifice, or visible acts of charity. Yet it reaches directly into the place where love either grows or dies. The smallest practice may be carrying us toward the deepest thing.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Keep my heart clear.*

**Breathing out:** *Let no evil grow toward my neighbor.*
