## Day 94 — The Courage to Remain Watchful

### Abba Joseph of Panepho

As Abba Joseph of Panepho approached death, several elders gathered around him. He had spent many years in prayer and spiritual discipline, and his life was drawing to a close. While the others sat nearby, Joseph looked toward the window and saw the devil sitting there. Rather than becoming frightened, he called to his disciple and asked him to bring his walking stick.

Joseph explained that the visitor seemed to think his old age had left him too weak to resist temptation. Taking hold of his stick, he demonstrated that he had not surrendered his strength or his resolve. The elders watching him saw the devil flee through the window like a frightened dog. Even at the threshold of death, Joseph remained unwilling to abandon the watchfulness that had shaped his life.

### Devotional

We often imagine that spiritual maturity will eventually bring us to a place where struggle is no longer necessary. We hope that years of practice will quiet our restless desires, heal our wounded habits, and free us from the temptations that once seemed so powerful. Surely there must come a time when we can finally declare the work finished. Joseph's story suggests that the spiritual journey may not unfold quite that way.

There is something almost humorous about the image of an elderly monk reaching for his walking stick to confront the devil. Joseph does not appear overwhelmed by the threat before him, nor does he seem surprised that temptation has arrived so late in his life. He simply recognizes what is happening and prepares to respond. His confidence is not that temptation has disappeared, but that he need not surrender to it.

The desert tradition frequently portrays temptation as a struggle against external spiritual forces. Modern readers may understand such encounters differently, seeing them as images of the fears, desires, and destructive patterns that arise within the human heart. We need not settle every question about the nature of Joseph's visitor to recognize the wisdom of his response. Whatever form temptation takes, its presence does not require our cooperation.

This distinction becomes especially important when we have struggled with the same patterns for many years. We may grow discouraged when an old resentment returns, an abandoned habit begins calling again, or a familiar fear interrupts our peace. We wonder whether our previous efforts accomplished anything at all. Yet the reappearance of a struggle does not mean that the growth we experienced was an illusion.

Perhaps maturity is better understood as a changing relationship with our struggles rather than their complete disappearance. A thought that once consumed an entire afternoon may now pass within minutes. A provocation that once led to anger may become an opportunity for patience. The circumstances may appear familiar, but something within us has learned to respond differently.

Still, watchfulness need not mean living in constant suspicion of ourselves. There is a kind of vigilance rooted in fear, always searching for evidence that we are about to fail. Such attention can become exhausting, turning the spiritual life into an endless confrontation with our own weakness. Joseph's response suggests another possibility: the quiet readiness of someone who knows that difficulty may arise but does not need to fear its arrival.

His walking stick offers a fitting image for this kind of readiness. It is an ordinary object, something that supports an aging body and helps it continue along the path. Perhaps our own forms of support are equally ordinary: a moment of silence, a trusted friendship, a familiar prayer, or the simple decision to begin again. We do not always need extraordinary strength to remain faithful to what matters.

Joseph's story invites us to abandon the expectation that spiritual freedom must mean the absence of struggle. We can grow older, become wiser, and experience genuine transformation while still encountering the unfinished places within us. The presence of temptation need not be a verdict on our progress, nor must every difficulty become a battle against ourselves. Sometimes faithfulness is simply remembering that we remain free to choose our next response.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Let me meet each moment with awareness.*

**Breathing out:** *Give me courage to choose what is good.*
