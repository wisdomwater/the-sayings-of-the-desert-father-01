## Day 71 — Making Use of Adversity

### Amma Theodora

Amma Theodora once asked Archbishop Theophilus what it means to make good use of the circumstances we are given. He answered that every season can become spiritually fruitful, even the ones we would never have chosen. When life becomes difficult, patience and humility can transform the difficulty into a place of growth. When shame or humiliation comes, willing acceptance can keep the experience from being wasted.

The teaching does not say that adversity is good in itself or that suffering should be sought. It says that circumstances do not have the final word over what they produce in us. Even what opposes us can become material for formation. The question becomes not only what is happening, but how we will meet what is happening.

### Devotional

Much of life arrives without asking permission. Plans change, people disappoint us, bodies become tired, work becomes difficult, and circumstances narrow our choices. Theodora begins with the reality that we do not control every season we enter.

Our first instinct is often to ask how quickly we can escape what is unpleasant. Sometimes leaving or changing the situation is wise and necessary. But there are also difficulties that cannot simply be removed, and then another kind of freedom becomes possible.

Theodora asks what the experience might become in us. Hardship can make us bitter, frightened, defensive, or closed, but those outcomes are not inevitable. Patience creates enough space for another response to emerge.

Humility also changes adversity. It loosens the demand that life unfold according to our expectations or that we always appear competent and secure. We become more able to inhabit reality as it is rather than continually arguing with the fact that it is not what we wanted.

This does not mean passivity. Acceptance is not the same as approving injustice, tolerating abuse, or refusing to change what can be changed. It means beginning from what is actually true so that our response can arise from clarity rather than resistance alone.

There are seasons when spiritual practice looks less like achievement and more like endurance. We may have little energy for insight, progress, or impressive discipline. Simply remaining patient, honest, and open can become the work of the day.

Adversity can also reveal what easier seasons conceal. Our need for control, approval, certainty, or comfort becomes clearer when those things are threatened. What is exposed can then be met with compassion and brought into prayer.

Theodora does not promise that every difficulty will make sense. She offers something quieter: nothing has to be spiritually useless. Even within circumstances we would never choose, there may still be a way to become more patient, humble, awake, and free.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Meet me in what I cannot change.*

**Breathing out:** *Form patience within me.*
