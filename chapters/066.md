## Day 66 — The Same Work

### Abba Poemen

Abba Poemen imagined three people living very different lives. One practices stillness faithfully, another is ill and gives thanks to God, and the third serves others with a pure heart. Their circumstances and activities appear almost nothing alike. Yet Poemen says that all three are doing the same work.

He refuses to rank contemplation above weakness or service above silence. Each person receives a different situation and responds faithfully within it. What makes the work spiritual is not its outward form alone, but the orientation of the heart within it. A quiet cell, a sickbed, and a life of service can each become places of communion with God.

### Devotional

We often carry an image of what spiritual life is supposed to look like. Prayer, silence, study, and contemplation may seem more obviously sacred than weakness, interruption, or ordinary service. Poemen widens the field considerably.

The first person has the life we might most easily recognize as spiritual. He practices stillness and deliberately turns his attention toward God. There is discipline, intention, and space for prayer in his way of living. Yet Poemen does not place him above the others.

The second person can do very little. Illness has taken away much of the activity that might otherwise have made her feel useful or faithful. Her practice is simply to receive her limitation without abandoning gratitude.

That is a demanding form of spiritual work. Gratitude in weakness does not require pretending that pain is pleasant or that limitation is desirable. It means remaining open to God even when life no longer allows us to serve, accomplish, or practice as we once could.

The third person may have almost no silence at all. His days are spent responding to other people and attending to practical needs. If that service comes from a clear and loving heart, Poemen says it belongs to the same work as contemplation.

This prevents us from dividing life too sharply into sacred and ordinary activities. Prayer can happen in stillness, but it can also take the form of receiving help graciously or giving help without resentment. Different seasons may call forth different expressions of the same faithfulness.

There is also freedom here for times when our circumstances change. A person accustomed to action may someday have to receive care, while someone who has enjoyed solitude may suddenly be needed by others. The spiritual task is not to preserve one preferred form of life at all costs.

Poemen asks us instead to discover what faithfulness looks like where we actually are. Some days it will mean sitting quietly before God, some days accepting our limits with gratitude, and some days setting aside our own plans to serve another person. The outward work changes, while the deeper movement remains the same.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Meet me where I am.*

**Breathing out:** *Make this moment faithful.*
