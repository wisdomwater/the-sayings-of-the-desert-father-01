## Day 64 — The Work That Comes First

### Abba Theodore of Pherme

A brother asked Abba Theodore what he meant by the work of the soul. Theodore said that whatever is done in obedience to God belongs to that deeper work, while labor done merely for one’s own purposes is secondary. He then gave a simple example: if someone is sick and needs a visit, finishing our own work first may cause us to postpone love until it never happens.

He gave another example of a brother asking for help with his own work. If we refuse because we are too occupied with our own task, Theodore says we may be placing what is secondary above what matters most. The real work is not always the thing already in our hands.

### Devotional

Busyness can create a strong sense of importance. A full schedule, unfinished task, or demanding project can make everything else feel like an interruption. Theodore asks whether the interruption may sometimes be the real work.

This is difficult because our own tasks are usually easier to measure. We know when the report is finished, the floor is swept, the email is sent, or the project is complete. Love is less tidy and rarely fits neatly into a checklist.

Theodore does not despise ordinary work. The monks depended on manual labor, and he knew that responsibilities had to be carried out. His warning is against allowing productivity to become more sacred than relationship.

There are moments when another person’s need asks something inconvenient from us. We may genuinely intend to help later, after the current task is finished and the schedule becomes easier. Later has a way of becoming never.

The work of the soul often appears without appointment. Someone needs attention, encouragement, patience, forgiveness, or simple presence. These moments can look inefficient precisely because love is not optimized for speed.

Discernment is still necessary. We cannot respond to every request, and compassion without boundaries can become another form of disorder. Theodore’s point is not that every interruption must be obeyed, but that our own agenda should not automatically outrank the person before us.

This teaching also reaches into spiritual practice. We can become so committed to our routines, reading, prayer, or discipline that we resent the people who disturb them. A practice meant to form love has then begun competing with love.

Theodore brings us back to purpose. Work matters because it serves life, not because activity itself is holy. When the task becomes more important than the person, something has quietly been reversed.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Show me the work that matters most.*

**Breathing out:** *Make me available to love.*
