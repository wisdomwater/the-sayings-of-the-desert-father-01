## Day 62 — Use the Strength You Have

### Abba Achilles

Abba Ammoes and Abba Bitimios visited Abba Achilles and found that he had been working through the night. He had woven far more than he actually needed. When they asked why, Achilles said he feared being asked why he had not worked when he still had strength available.

His answer was not an argument for endless productivity. Achilles simply wanted to give himself fully to the work that was presently within his capacity. He did what he could with the strength he had, neither pretending to possess more nor choosing to use less.

### Devotional

We often compare our actual strength with an imagined version of ourselves. We think about what we should be able to accomplish, how disciplined we ought to be, or how much energy someone else seems to have. That comparison can make the strength we do possess feel insignificant.

Achilles begins somewhere more concrete. He works with the capacity that is actually available to him. The question is not whether he can do everything, but whether he is faithfully using what he has.

This can be freeing when our energy is limited. Some days we are capable of sustained effort, while other days fatigue, illness, grief, or ordinary human limitation narrows what is possible. Faithfulness does not require pretending those differences are unreal.

The danger lies at both extremes. We can demand more from ourselves than we have to give and call that devotion, or we can withdraw from what is possible because we cannot do as much as we wish. Achilles points toward a steadier middle path.

The work itself may be small. We may answer one message, take one walk, offer one prayer, finish one task, or show up for one conversation with attention. What matters is that the available strength is offered rather than despised.

There is humility in this approach. We accept that our capacity changes and that we are not sovereign over our bodies, circumstances, or energy. At the same time, we remain responsible for what is genuinely within our reach.

This also keeps effort from becoming performance. Achilles was not producing more in order to impress anyone or secure an identity as a hard worker. His labor was hidden, simple, and responsive to the strength he happened to have.

The deeper spiritual practice is learning to notice capacity without turning it into judgment. We can ask what is possible today without immediately comparing the answer to yesterday or tomorrow. Then effort becomes an act of attention rather than accusation.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Show me the strength I have.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to use it well.*
