## Day 49 — Mercy Toward Everyone

### Abba Pambo

Abba Theodore of Pherme came to Abba Pambo and asked him for a word. Pambo was reluctant to speak quickly, but at last he gave him a simple instruction. He told Theodore to go and show mercy to everyone.

Pambo added that mercy gives a person confidence before God. He did not offer a complicated spiritual program or a demanding ascetic rule. He directed Theodore toward the way he would treat the people placed before him.

### Devotional

We often expect spiritual wisdom to be more complicated than this. We look for practices, insights, disciplines, and explanations that will help us become more faithful. Pambo reduces the matter to mercy.

His instruction is not selective. He does not say to show mercy only to those who deserve it, understand us, agree with us, or have treated us well. He says to extend it to everyone. That word makes the teaching much more difficult than it first appears.

Mercy is easy to admire in the abstract. It becomes harder when the person before us is irritating, unfair, selfish, or responsible for real harm. Our instinct is often to decide whether they have earned gentleness before we offer it. Pambo places mercy earlier in the process.

This does not mean ignoring what is wrong. Mercy can exist alongside boundaries, consequences, truth-telling, and refusal to participate in harmful behavior. It does not require calling evil good or pretending that pain did not occur. It asks us not to let another person's failure become permission for cruelty.

That distinction matters because judgment can change us. We may begin by recognizing that someone acted badly and end by imagining that they are nothing more than the worst thing they have done. Anger simplifies people until their complexity disappears. Mercy resists that reduction.

To show mercy is to remember that the person before us remains unfinished. They carry wounds, habits, fears, contradictions, and possibilities that we cannot fully see. This does not excuse their choices, but it keeps us from imagining that we possess the whole truth about them. Mercy leaves room for repentance and change.

Pambo also connects mercy with confidence before God. Perhaps this confidence grows because mercy brings us into alignment with the character of the God we hope to meet. We come before God asking to be seen in our fullness rather than reduced to our failures. The mercy we desire begins to shape the mercy we offer.

This does not make mercy a transaction. We are not kind to others so that God will owe us kindness in return. Rather, receiving mercy and giving mercy belong to the same movement of grace. A heart that knows its own dependence on compassion becomes less eager to deny compassion to someone else.

Showing mercy to everyone also means noticing the quiet forms of harshness. We may never openly condemn someone, yet we can dismiss them internally, enjoy their embarrassment, repeat their failure, or quietly hope that they receive what we think they deserve. These movements may remain invisible to others. They still shape the kind of heart we are forming.

Mercy asks us to interrupt that formation. When resentment begins to harden, we can remember our own need for patience. When another person's weakness irritates us, we can remember how slowly some parts of our own lives have changed. Compassion often begins with memory.

There will also be people from whom distance is necessary. We may forgive someone without restoring trust, wish them well without continuing the relationship, or refuse access while still refusing hatred. Mercy does not require returning ourselves to danger. It requires guarding the heart even while we guard the boundary.

Pambo's instruction is therefore simple without being easy. We do not need to solve every mystery of the spiritual life before practicing it. We can begin with the person in front of us and ask what mercy would look like here. Over time, that repeated question may shape us more deeply than many impressive practices.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Let me receive your mercy.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to show it to everyone.*
