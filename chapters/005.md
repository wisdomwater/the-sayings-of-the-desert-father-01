## Day 5 — A Little of Every Virtue

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John taught that a person should not try to build a life around only one virtue. Instead, when morning came, he encouraged his brothers to begin again in all of them, taking up a little patience, a little humility, prayer, watchfulness, and care in speech. The spiritual life was not one narrow road but a whole pattern of becoming.

He urged them to refuse injury without returning injury, to avoid measuring the faults of others, to work quietly, endure difficulty, practice discernment, and repent when they wandered. He wanted them to remember that life is brief and that there is no need to wait until one virtue has been perfected before beginning another. Each morning, they could simply take up a little of everything that makes the heart whole.

### Devotional

There is a part of us that likes dramatic transformation. We imagine finding the one practice, insight, discipline, or breakthrough that will finally change everything. If we could only discover the right method and apply ourselves intensely enough, perhaps the scattered pieces of our lives would finally fall into place.

John offers something much less dramatic and, perhaps, much more realistic. Take a little of every virtue. His original list is almost overwhelming in its breadth, as though he refuses to reduce the spiritual life to a single technique.

Prayer matters, but so does humility. Patience matters, but so does watchfulness. Restraint, gentleness, repentance, discernment, labor, and endurance all belong together because human beings are complicated, and becoming whole may require many small movements in many directions at once.

There is wisdom here for those of us who are inclined to optimize ourselves. We can turn even spirituality into a project, identifying our weakest area, building a plan, measuring progress, and imagining that once the defect is corrected we will finally become the person we ought to be. Yet life rarely develops so neatly.

While we are learning patience, we discover that we also need humility. While cultivating silence, we realize that silence without courage can become avoidance. While practicing restraint, we may discover that restraint without compassion can harden into coldness.

The virtues seem to grow together like plants in the same garden. Their roots intertwine beneath the surface, each strengthening or exposing something in the others. Growth in one area often reveals work still waiting nearby.

Perhaps this is why John’s counsel begins with the morning. He is not asking us to complete anything; he is asking us to begin. Today there may be one conversation in which we can listen more carefully, one irritation we can meet without reacting, one task we can do attentively, or one weakness in another person that we choose not to judge.

None of these moments will feel heroic. They may be so ordinary that no one else notices them at all. But perhaps the hidden life of virtue is built precisely from these small choices.

Holiness may be less like constructing a monument and more like tending a garden. We water here, prune there, pull a weed, protect a young shoot, and return tomorrow to see what needs attention next. No single act makes the garden flourish, yet over time something living begins to take shape.

John’s counsel also protects us from perfectionism. We do not have to become perfectly patient before practicing generosity, or perfectly disciplined before learning mercy. We are allowed to be beginners in many virtues at once, growing unevenly and returning to what has been neglected.

Morning comes, and the invitation is wonderfully modest. Take up a little patience, a little courage, a little mercy, and a little prayer. Then tomorrow, without pretending the work is finished, begin again.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Teach me the next small good.*

**Breathing out:** *Let it take root in me.*
