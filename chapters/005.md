## Day 5 — A Little of Every Virtue

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John said that he did not want a person to build a life around only one virtue.

Instead, when morning came, he encouraged his brothers to begin again in all of them.

Practice a little patience. A little humility. Attend to prayer. Guard the tongue and the eyes. Refuse to answer injury with injury. Do not spend the day measuring the faults of others. Work quietly. Endure difficulty. Practice discernment. Repent when you wander. Remember that life is brief.

Do not wait until you have perfected one thing before beginning another.

Each morning, take up a little of everything that makes the heart whole.

### Devotional

There is a part of us that likes dramatic transformation. We imagine finding the one practice, insight, discipline, or breakthrough that will finally change everything. If we could only discover the right method and apply ourselves intensely enough, perhaps the scattered pieces of our lives would suddenly fall into place.

John offers something much less dramatic.

Take a little of every virtue.

His original list is almost overwhelming. Prayer, humility, patience, watchfulness, purity of speech, gentleness, fasting, repentance, discernment, manual labor, endurance—the practices tumble over one another as though John is unwilling to reduce the spiritual life to a single technique. Human beings are complicated, and becoming whole may require many small movements in many directions.

There is wisdom here for those of us who are inclined to optimize ourselves.

We can turn even spirituality into a project. We identify our weakest area, develop a plan, measure our progress, and imagine that once we have corrected the defect we will become the person we ought to be. Yet life rarely develops so neatly. While we are learning patience, we discover that we also need humility. While cultivating silence, we realize we need courage. While practicing restraint, we learn that restraint without compassion can become hardness.

The virtues seem to grow together like plants in the same garden.

Perhaps this is why John's advice begins in the morning. He is not asking us to complete anything. He is asking us to begin. Today there may be one conversation in which we can listen more carefully. One irritation we can meet without immediately reacting. One moment when we can tell the truth instead of protecting our image. One task we can do attentively. One person whose weakness we can choose not to judge.

None of these moments will feel heroic.

But perhaps holiness is less like constructing a monument and more like tending a garden. We water here, prune there, pull a weed, protect a young shoot, and return tomorrow to discover what needs attention next. No single act makes the garden flourish, yet over time something living takes shape.

John's counsel also protects us from perfectionism. We do not have to become perfectly patient before practicing generosity, nor perfectly disciplined before learning mercy. We can be beginners in many virtues at once.

Morning comes, and the invitation is wonderfully modest.

Take a little patience.

A little courage.

A little mercy.

A little prayer.

And then, tomorrow, begin again.
