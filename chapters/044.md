## Day 44 — Without Anxiety of Spirit

### Abba Ammonas

One day Abba Ammonas came to a river crossing and sat down to wait for the public ferry. Another boat arrived sooner, and the people there urged him to cross with them instead. Ammonas declined and continued waiting for the ordinary boat he had intended to take.

While he waited, he took palm branches in his hands and began weaving them together. Then he undid the work and started again, passing the time without allowing impatience to take hold. When the ferry finally came, he crossed in peace and explained that he had done this so that he might walk without anxiety of spirit.

### Devotional

There is a particular kind of anxiety that comes from believing everything should happen faster. We begin with a simple preference, but soon the delay feels personal, wasteful, or intolerable. The mind starts searching for another route, another option, or some way to force the moment forward.

Ammonas does something surprisingly gentle with that impulse. He is offered a quicker way across the river, but he does not seize it merely because it is faster. Instead, he stays with the path he had already chosen and gives his attention to the small work in his hands. The waiting becomes part of the journey rather than an obstacle to it.

This is not a rejection of efficiency. There are times when taking the faster route is entirely sensible, and there is nothing inherently spiritual about unnecessary delay. Ammonas is responding to something happening within himself, not establishing a rule about ferries. He is protecting the peace of his spirit from the habit of anxious urgency.

That habit is familiar in modern life. We refresh the page, check the message, watch the clock, reroute the drive, and become irritated when events refuse to move at the speed we prefer. Even minor delays can begin to feel like evidence that something is going wrong. The body may remain still while the mind races several steps ahead.

Ammonas brings attention back to what is actually here. There are palm branches in his hands, a river in front of him, and a boat that will come when it comes. He does not need the next moment to arrive before he can inhabit this one. His peace does not depend on shortening the wait.

The weaving and unweaving are especially beautiful. He is not even using the time to produce something useful. The activity gives his hands a rhythm and his mind somewhere simple to rest. He allows the repeated motion to carry him through the delay without converting the delay into frustration.

This challenges our instinct to justify waiting through productivity. If we are delayed, we often feel compelled to make the time useful so that nothing is lost. We answer messages, organize tasks, or search for something else to accomplish. Ammonas suggests that time need not always produce something in order to be received well.

There is freedom in that. We can wait without treating waiting as failure. We can allow a slow process to remain slow, a conversation to unfold in its own time, or an uncertain situation to remain unresolved for a while. Not every unfinished thing requires immediate intervention.

This does not mean passivity. Some situations require action, persistence, or urgency, especially when another person’s well-being is at stake. The question is whether our movement is coming from clarity or from the discomfort of not being able to control the pace. Anxiety often disguises itself as necessity.

Ammonas gives us another possibility. We can act when action is needed and wait when waiting is required. We can keep our attention near the life that is actually happening instead of continually leaning into the next moment. Peace becomes possible when we stop demanding that time obey us.

Perhaps this is part of hospitality too. To be present to another person, we often have to surrender our preferred pace. People heal, speak, decide, grieve, and change according to rhythms we cannot command. Love sometimes waits beside them without trying to hurry the crossing.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Settle my anxious spirit.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to walk in peace.*
