## Day 75 — Correct Without Humiliating

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John was traveling by night with several brothers when the one guiding them lost the way. The others quietly asked John what they should do, afraid they might wander until they died. John realized that telling the guide directly could leave him ashamed before everyone. So he said he was too weak to continue and would remain there until morning.

The other brothers chose to stay with John rather than go on without him. By morning they could find their way again without publicly exposing the guide’s mistake. John solved the practical problem while protecting the brother from unnecessary humiliation. His concern was not only getting everyone safely home, but also preserving the dignity of the person who had failed.

### Devotional

Mistakes create vulnerable moments. When someone is already aware that they have failed, correction can easily become humiliation. John pays attention to both the problem and the person standing inside it.

He does not pretend that being lost is unimportant. The group really does need to stop, because continuing could make the situation worse. But he finds a way to address the danger without turning the guide into the object of everyone’s frustration.

This kind of mercy requires imagination. The easiest response would have been to announce the mistake and take control. John instead asks how the same practical outcome might be reached without adding shame.

There are many moments when we face the same choice. Someone forgets something, makes a poor decision, misunderstands instructions, or fails publicly. We may be able to correct the situation while still protecting their dignity.

Protecting dignity does not mean avoiding accountability. Sometimes a mistake must be named clearly, especially when harm has been done or responsibility matters. But clarity and humiliation are not the same thing.

Shame often makes correction harder to receive. Once a person feels exposed, attention shifts from learning to self-protection. Gentleness can preserve enough safety for truth to be heard.

John also shows that compassion can cost us something. He accepts inconvenience, delay, and the appearance of weakness so that another person does not have to carry public embarrassment. Mercy sometimes means taking on a little discomfort ourselves.

The story leaves us with a different question when someone fails. Instead of asking only how to fix the mistake, we can also ask how to protect the person while we fix it. Truth does not become less truthful when it is carried with tenderness.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Give me gentleness with another’s weakness.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me to correct without shame.*
