# Day 13 — You Do Not Have to Enter Everything

### Abba Arsenius

Abba Arsenius said that when a monk traveled away from his cell, he should be careful not to become entangled in everything happening around him.

He did not need to enter every conversation.

He did not need to involve himself in every dispute.

He did not need to make every concern his own.

If he could move through the world without being pulled into everything he encountered, he would preserve his peace.

### Devotional

Some forms of distraction are obvious. We know when we are wasting time, endlessly scrolling, or avoiding something important. But other distractions arrive disguised as responsibility.

Something is happening, therefore we must have an opinion. Someone is arguing, therefore we must decide who is right. A controversy appears, therefore we must read about it, think about it, discuss it, and perhaps carry it with us for the rest of the day.

Arsenius offers a startling alternative. You do not have to enter everything. This is not indifference. There are moments when love requires involvement. Someone may genuinely need our help, our voice, our courage, or our presence. Peace that depends upon ignoring suffering is not the peace Arsenius is describing. But neither is constant involvement the same thing as love.

Sometimes we become entangled simply because something has captured our attention. We begin following a conflict that does not concern us. We feel compelled to correct someone whose opinion poses no real danger. We carry arguments between people we have never met. Before long, our interior life is crowded with matters over which we have little responsibility and even less control.

The modern world makes this almost effortless. A device in our pocket can present us with hundreds of situations each day and quietly imply that each one deserves an emotional response. Outrage becomes participation. Awareness begins to feel like obligation.

Yet attention is not unlimited. Every concern we carry occupies some portion of the heart. Arsenius seems to understand that peace requires discernment about what we allow to enter. Not everything we can know needs to be known. Not everything we can answer needs an answer. Not every invitation to react deserves a yes.

There is freedom in asking a simple question:

*Is this mine to carry?*

Sometimes the answer will be yes. Then we should enter fully, with courage and love. But sometimes the answer will be no. We can bless what we cannot carry and let it pass beyond us.

This is not withdrawal from the world. It may actually allow us to be more present to the part of the world entrusted to us: the person in front of us, the work before us, the suffering we can actually relieve, the relationship that genuinely requires our attention.

Peace is not found by controlling everything outside us. It may begin when we stop allowing everything outside us to take up residence within.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Show me what is mine to carry.*

**Breathing out:** *Help me release the rest.*
