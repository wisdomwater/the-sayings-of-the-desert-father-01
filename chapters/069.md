## Day 69 — Mary Still Needs Martha

### Abba Silvanus

A brother came to Abba Silvanus and saw the monks working hard with their hands. He quoted Jesus about food that perishes and reminded Silvanus that Mary had chosen the better part. Silvanus quietly gave him a book and placed him alone in an empty cell to read. When the usual mealtime came, no one summoned him.

Eventually the hungry brother went looking for Silvanus and asked why he had not been called to eat. Silvanus replied that a spiritual man like him should have no need for ordinary food, while the brothers had worked because they were fleshly and needed to eat. The brother understood the lesson and asked forgiveness. Silvanus told him that Mary still needs Martha, because contemplation is sustained by ordinary work.

### Devotional

It is easy to divide life into higher and lower kinds of activity. Prayer, contemplation, reading, and silence can seem spiritually important, while cooking, cleaning, earning money, repairing things, and tending practical needs can feel merely necessary. Silvanus refuses that division.

The visiting brother is not wrong to value contemplation. His mistake is imagining that contemplation floats free from the material life that supports it. Someone grows the food, prepares the meal, repairs the roof, and carries the water.

Silvanus does not resolve the tension by saying Martha is better than Mary. He keeps both together. Attention to God and attention to ordinary responsibility belong within the same life.

This matters whenever spirituality becomes a subtle escape from embodiment. We may prefer reading about compassion to doing the inconvenient work compassion requires. We may love silence while resenting the chores that make a shared life possible.

Ordinary labor can itself become part of contemplation. Hands washing dishes, typing reports, folding clothes, or preparing meals can remain attentive to God. The work does not have to stop being practical in order to become sacred.

There is also humility in recognizing how dependent we are on the work of others. Much of what allows us to pray, study, rest, and reflect has already been made possible by someone else's labor. Gratitude begins when we notice those hidden contributions.

The saying also protects work from becoming an idol. Martha needs Mary just as Mary needs Martha, because productivity without inward attention can become empty motion. Work and contemplation correct one another when neither is allowed to claim the whole life.

Silvanus invites us into a spirituality that does not despise the ordinary. We can seek God in silence and then return to the tasks waiting for our hands. The same presence can accompany us in both places.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Meet me in stillness.*

**Breathing out:** *Meet me in the work.*
