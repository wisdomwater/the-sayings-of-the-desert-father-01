## Day 6 — I Cannot Choose for You

### Abba Poemen

A brother came to Abba Poemen carrying a question.

He had received an inheritance and did not know what he should do with it.

Poemen did not answer immediately.

“Go,” he told the brother. “Come back in three days, and I will tell you.”

Three days later the brother returned.

Poemen considered the possibilities with him. He could tell him to give the money to the church. He could tell him to give it to his relatives. He could tell him to give it away to the poor.

But Poemen would not turn any of these possibilities into a command.

In the end he said, “Do what you wish. This decision is yours.”

### Devotional

We often seek wisdom when what we really want is certainty.

A difficult decision stands before us, and we imagine that somewhere there must be a person wise enough to tell us exactly what to do. If we can find the right teacher, pastor, counselor, book, or spiritual guide, perhaps they can relieve us of the burden of choosing.

The brother comes to Poemen in precisely this position.

His question sounds simple: *What should I do with the money?* But Poemen refuses to make the decision for him.

That refusal is striking. A less careful spiritual teacher might have enjoyed the authority of prescribing an answer. Give it all away. Give it to the church. Keep only what you need. Any of these could sound impressively spiritual.

Poemen does something harder.

He gives the brother his freedom back.

He even allows the question to remain unresolved for three days. Perhaps discernment sometimes needs this empty space. Not every decision improves when we immediately fill the uncertainty with an answer. Sometimes wisdom begins by allowing ourselves to remain with the question long enough to notice what is moving beneath it.

When the brother returns, Poemen names possibilities without pretending that any formula can remove the responsibility of choosing. There may be good reasons for several paths. There may also be mixed motives hiding inside every one of them.

Eventually, the brother must choose.

We may find this uncomfortable because freedom carries responsibility with it. If someone else tells us what to do, then some part of the burden belongs to them. But when a wise person says, *I cannot choose this for you*, we are brought back into direct contact with our own conscience.

Perhaps good spiritual guidance does not create dependence. It helps us become capable of discernment.

There are times when right and wrong are clear. But much of life takes place in more ambiguous territory: whether to stay or leave, speak or remain silent, give something away or preserve it, accept an opportunity or decline it. In those places, faithfulness cannot always be reduced to a rule.

We listen.

We wait.

We become honest about our desires and fears. We seek wisdom from others without asking them to become responsible for our lives. And then, eventually, we choose.

Poemen's restraint may itself be the teaching.

A true spiritual guide does not need to become the voice of God in another person's life. Sometimes love offers wisdom and then steps aside.

The final step belongs to us.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Give me wisdom to listen.*

**Breathing out:** *Give me courage to choose.*
