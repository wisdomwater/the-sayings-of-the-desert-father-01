## Day 6 — I Cannot Choose for You

### Abba Poemen

A brother once came to Abba Poemen after receiving an inheritance and asked what he should do with it. Poemen did not answer immediately. Instead, he told the brother to go away and return after three days.

When the brother came back, Poemen considered several possibilities with him. The money could be given to the church, shared with relatives, or distributed among the poor. Each option could be defended as good, yet Poemen refused to turn any of them into a command.

In the end, he gave the decision back to the brother. He would not choose in his place or pretend that wisdom required only one possible answer. The brother would have to discern what to do and then take responsibility for the choice.

### Devotional

We often seek wisdom when what we really want is certainty. Faced with an important decision, we may long for someone wiser, more experienced, or more spiritual to tell us exactly what to do. If they choose for us, perhaps we can be spared the anxiety of choosing badly.

Poemen refuses to offer that kind of relief. He listens, considers the possibilities, and then gives the responsibility back. His guidance does not remove freedom; it makes room for it.

The three-day pause may be as important as the answer itself. Poemen does not react immediately, and he does not encourage the brother to resolve the matter simply to escape the discomfort of uncertainty. Sometimes discernment requires allowing a question to remain open long enough for our first urgency to settle.

We may find this difficult because ambiguity can feel unsafe. A rule gives us something solid to hold, while discernment asks us to listen more carefully to circumstance, conscience, wisdom, desire, responsibility, and love. Often several genuinely good possibilities remain before us.

This is where spiritual guidance can become distorted. We may unconsciously ask a teacher, pastor, friend, book, or tradition to take responsibility for our lives. Instead of asking for help seeing clearly, we ask someone else to make the choice so that we will not have to bear its uncertainty.

Good guidance does something different. It helps us see more clearly without making us dependent on the guide. It offers wisdom, raises questions, names dangers, and perhaps reveals possibilities we had not considered, but eventually it allows us to stand before our own life again.

There are, of course, moral choices that do not require endless discernment. Cruelty does not become wise because we have reflected on it for three days, and love does not become optional simply because obedience is inconvenient. Yet much of life is not arranged so neatly.

We choose between two good opportunities, two legitimate responsibilities, or two paths whose consequences cannot be known in advance. At such moments, even sincere prayer may not produce certainty. We may still have to choose without knowing how the story will unfold.

Poemen seems willing to trust the brother with that burden. He does not need to become the voice of God in someone else’s life. He can offer wisdom and then step aside.

Perhaps freedom is itself part of our formation. We learn not merely by making correct choices, but by becoming people capable of choosing with greater honesty, humility, and love. Even when a decision later proves imperfect, we can learn, adjust, and continue.

The spiritual life does not always give us an answer.

Sometimes it gives us enough light to choose.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Give me wisdom to listen.*

**Breathing out:** *Give me courage to choose.*
