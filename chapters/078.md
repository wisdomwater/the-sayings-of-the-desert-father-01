## Day 78 — A Little of Every Virtue

### Abba Poemen

A brother asked Abba Poemen whether a person could rely on one single spiritual practice. Poemen answered by recalling the wisdom of Abba John the Dwarf. John did not want to build his whole life around one virtue alone. He preferred to take a little from all the virtues.

The saying resists the temptation to reduce spiritual life to one strength, discipline, or method. A person may pray deeply yet neglect patience, practice generosity while avoiding humility, or pursue silence without learning kindness. Poemen points instead toward a life whose different virtues grow together. Wholeness matters more than excelling in one isolated practice.

### Devotional

There is comfort in finding one thing we are good at. Once we discover a practice that seems fruitful, we may begin to treat it as the answer to everything. Poemen gently unsettles that instinct.

Spiritual life is wider than any single discipline. Prayer matters, but prayer alone does not excuse cruelty, impatience, or dishonesty. Generosity matters, but generosity does not remove the need for humility and self-knowledge.

Different virtues also correct one another. Courage without gentleness can become aggression, while gentleness without courage can become avoidance. Discipline without mercy can become harshness, while mercy without discernment can lose its way.

This is why growth often feels uneven. We may discover that one part of ourselves has matured while another remains strangely undeveloped. Poemen does not ask us to despise that imbalance, but to notice what has been neglected.

A little of every virtue also suggests patience. We do not have to perfect everything at once or become impressive in every dimension of life. Small acts of faithfulness repeated across many areas can gradually shape a more integrated person.

There is humility in this approach because it keeps us from building identity around our strongest trait. The generous person still has something to learn about restraint, and the disciplined person still has something to learn about compassion. No single strength becomes permission to stop growing elsewhere.

The saying also protects us from spiritual specialization. We are not meant to become merely contemplative, merely active, merely disciplined, or merely compassionate. The deeper invitation is toward a life in which these qualities begin to support one another.

Poemen leaves us with a broad and spacious vision of formation. We can pray a little, serve a little, forgive a little, restrain ourselves a little, listen a little, and begin again a little. Over time, those small movements may become a life that is whole.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Shape every part of me.*

**Breathing out:** *Teach me the fullness of virtue.*
