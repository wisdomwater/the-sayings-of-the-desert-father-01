## Day 59 — Strength for the Struggle

### Amma Sarah

For many years Amma Sarah was fiercely troubled by sexual temptation. She did not ask God to remove the struggle from her life. Instead, she prayed that God would give her strength to endure it faithfully.

The temptation continued, but Sarah kept refusing to surrender to it. Over time, the struggle itself became a place of endurance rather than defeat. Her prayer changed the center of attention from making the temptation disappear to becoming strong enough to remain free within its presence.

### Devotional

Most of us naturally want difficulty to end. When a temptation, fear, craving, grief, or recurring thought returns again and again, the most obvious prayer is for it to be taken away. We imagine freedom as the absence of struggle. Sarah imagines something more demanding.

She asks for strength instead of escape. That does not mean the temptation is good or that suffering should be romanticized. It means that a difficult interior experience does not have to disappear before we can begin becoming free. Freedom can start while the struggle is still present.

This changes the question we bring to prayer. Instead of asking only, “How do I make this stop?” we can also ask, “How do I remain grounded while this is here?” The first question seeks relief, while the second seeks capacity. Sometimes we need both.

Sarah’s approach also challenges the shame that often accompanies repeated temptation. When the same desire returns for the hundredth time, we may conclude that nothing has changed. Yet the presence of temptation is not the same as surrender to it. Growth may be happening in the way we meet what continues to arise.

There is a difference between being tempted and being ruled. An urge can appear without determining what happens next. A thought can enter the mind without becoming an instruction. A feeling can be intense without becoming a command.

Strength is often quieter than we expect. It may look like pausing for ten seconds, leaving a room, asking for help, praying one sentence, or simply refusing to take the next step toward the familiar pattern. These choices can feel unimpressive in the moment. Repeated over time, they become a form of freedom.

Sarah’s prayer also makes room for honesty. She does not pretend that the temptation is gone or that she has become immune to it. She acknowledges the battle without building her identity around it. The struggle is real, but it is not the whole of who she is.

There is humility in asking for strength. We admit that determination alone may not be enough and that we need grace, wisdom, support, and repeated return. We stop measuring ourselves by whether temptation appears and begin paying attention to how we respond. That shift can soften the cycle of failure and self-condemnation.

Sometimes the struggle will lessen. Sometimes it may remain for a long time, changing slowly or returning under stress. Sarah’s example does not promise a timetable. It offers a way to live faithfully even when relief is incomplete.

The deeper hope is not that we become people who never experience desire. It is that desire no longer has the final word. We can feel the pull, recognize what is happening, and still choose what leads toward life. Strength grows each time we discover that wanting something is not the same as having to obey it.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Give me strength for what is here.*

**Breathing out:** *Keep me free within the struggle.*
