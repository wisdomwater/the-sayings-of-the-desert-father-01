## Day 89 — The Freedom Not to Know

### Abba Pambo

People said of Abba Pambo that when he was asked to interpret Scripture or explain a spiritual saying, he did not answer quickly. He was willing to say that he did not know the meaning of what had been placed before him. If the questioner pressed him again, Pambo still would not invent an answer merely to satisfy the request. He allowed the question to remain open rather than filling the silence with words.

His restraint was especially striking because people came to him precisely because they considered him wise. He could easily have protected that reputation by always having something to say. Instead, Pambo treated truth as more important than appearing knowledgeable. Sometimes wisdom meant refusing to speak beyond what he actually understood.

### Devotional

There is pressure in many parts of life to have an answer. Questions arrive and we feel expected to respond quickly, confidently, and clearly. Saying that we do not know can feel like admitting inadequacy.

Pambo offers another picture of wisdom. He does not treat uncertainty as something shameful that must immediately be concealed. He gives himself permission to remain beside a question without pretending it has already yielded its answer.

This is particularly important in spiritual matters. Questions about God, suffering, Scripture, death, love, and human motives often reach beyond what can be neatly explained. Quick certainty may sometimes protect us from the discomfort of mystery more than it serves the truth.

There is humility in allowing our knowledge to have edges. We can know something deeply without knowing everything surrounding it. Naming the boundary honestly keeps conviction from turning into pretense.

Pambo's silence also suggests that not every question needs an immediate conclusion. Some things become clearer through time, prayer, experience, or simply living with them for a while. An unanswered question can continue working within us.

This does not mean abandoning thought or refusing to seek understanding. Pambo was not celebrating ignorance, but resisting the temptation to speak before understanding had ripened. Careful searching and honest uncertainty can belong together.

There is freedom here in ordinary conversation as well. We do not have to defend ourselves by producing opinions about every subject or pretending certainty when evidence is thin. Sometimes saying that we do not yet know creates space for genuine curiosity.

Pambo invites us to value truth more than the appearance of certainty. We can listen, search, wonder, and admit where our understanding ends. In that openness, not knowing can become the beginning of deeper attention rather than a failure to be wise.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Give me courage not to know.*

**Breathing out:** *Keep me open to what is true.*
