## Day 15 — Do Not Carry It Inside

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John once went to the church at Scetis.

There he heard some of the brothers arguing with one another.

When he returned to his cell, he did not enter immediately.

Instead, he walked around the cell three times.

Some brothers saw him and wondered what he was doing.

John explained:

“My ears were still full of the argument. I walked around so that I could clear them and enter my cell with my mind at peace.”

### Devotional

Some encounters end long before they leave us. The conversation is over. The other person has gone home. The meeting has ended. The message has been sent. But inwardly, we are still there. We replay what was said. We imagine better responses. We construct arguments we never had the chance to make. The body may have moved on, but the mind keeps circling the scene.

John understands this. What is striking is that the argument was not even his. He merely heard other people disputing, yet he recognized that their agitation had entered him. The noise outside had become noise inside.

So before entering his cell, he walked. Three times around. It is such a small act, but perhaps that is exactly why it matters.

John does not demand that his mind instantly become peaceful. He gives himself a transition. He allows what he has absorbed to settle before carrying it into the place of prayer. We may need such thresholds too.

Modern life asks us to move from one emotional environment to another almost without pause. We leave a difficult meeting and immediately enter the next one. We finish reading something upsetting and turn toward our family. We spend an hour in traffic, walk through the front door, and wonder why our patience is already thin.

The nervous system does not always change rooms as quickly as the body does. Something often needs to be released first. Perhaps we need to sit in the car for two minutes before going inside. Perhaps we need to walk around the block. Perhaps we need to breathe slowly, wash our hands, make tea, pray a psalm, or simply name what we are carrying.

*That conversation upset me.*

*I am still angry.*

*I am bringing someone else's anxiety with me.*

Awareness creates a small space between what happened and what happens next. Without that space, one person's frustration can become another person's wound. We carry tension from work into dinner, from an argument into prayer, from the news into sleep. What entered us accidentally begins shaping how we meet people who had nothing to do with it.

John's three circles become a kind of mercy. Not only toward himself, but toward everyone he will encounter next. Stillness sometimes requires more than closing the door. Sometimes we must first notice what we are bringing through it.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Show me what I am carrying.*

**Breathing out:** *Let me leave it here.*
