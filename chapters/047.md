# Day 47 — Begin With Your Neighbor

### Abba John the Dwarf

Abba John said that a house cannot be built from the top downward. It must begin with the foundation and rise from there. When the brothers asked what he meant, John told them that the foundation is our neighbor, whom we must first learn to love well.

For John, this was not a secondary part of the spiritual life. He believed that the commandments of Christ rested upon this foundation. Whatever else we build will remain unstable if our relationship with others is neglected.

### Devotional

It is possible to become very interested in spiritual things while remaining difficult to live with. We can study theology, pray faithfully, pursue silence, and develop disciplined practices while still carrying impatience, resentment, or indifference toward the people nearest to us. John asks us to look at the foundation before admiring the rest of the house.

This can be uncomfortable because spiritual practices are often easier to measure. We know whether we prayed, read, fasted, attended worship, or completed a particular discipline. Love is less tidy because it unfolds in relationships filled with misunderstanding, competing needs, irritation, and vulnerability. The neighbor introduces unpredictability into the spiritual life.

John refuses to let us avoid that difficulty. He does not say that prayer or solitude are unimportant, because his own life was deeply shaped by both. He says that whatever rises from those practices must ultimately rest upon love of neighbor. If our devotion continually makes us less patient, less merciful, or less available to others, we should examine what we are building.

The image of a foundation also suggests order. We sometimes want to begin with the most elevated spiritual experiences. We may seek contemplation, insight, mystical awareness, or some deeper understanding of God before learning how to speak gently to the person beside us. John brings us back to what is immediately available.

Our neighbor is concrete. The person before us has habits that may irritate us, needs that interrupt us, and perspectives that do not always fit neatly with our own. Loving an imagined humanity can be much easier than loving a particular human being. John places the work precisely there.

This does not mean becoming agreeable to everyone. Love may require disagreement, boundaries, difficult conversations, or distance from harmful behavior. The foundation is not keeping everyone pleased with us. It is learning to seek another person's good without allowing contempt to take root.

That work can reveal much about us. A difficult relationship can expose our need to control, our sensitivity to criticism, our impatience with weakness, or our desire to be recognized as right. These discoveries may feel like interruptions to spiritual growth, but John suggests they are part of the building process. The neighbor becomes one of the places where our interior life is made visible.

This also prevents spirituality from becoming an escape. We can sometimes turn toward prayer because God feels easier to love than people do. God does not interrupt us at dinner, misunderstand our intentions, repeat the same complaint, or leave dishes in the sink. Human relationships continually test whether the love we profess can take flesh.

The Christian story itself points in this direction. Love of God becomes embodied in love for human beings, and Christ repeatedly joins the two rather than allowing them to remain separate. Feeding, forgiving, visiting, welcoming, and serving become ways of encountering the divine. The vertical and horizontal dimensions of love belong to the same life.

John's image is therefore both simple and demanding. Before worrying about how high the spiritual house has risen, we can ask whether the foundation is becoming stronger. We can look at the relationships nearest to us and notice where patience, honesty, forgiveness, or attention may still be needed.

Most of that work will not look dramatic. It happens in conversations, interruptions, apologies, shared meals, restrained words, and small acts of care. These ordinary exchanges may seem far removed from the heights of spiritual life. John tells us they are where the house begins.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Teach me to love the one before me.*

**Breathing out:** *Build your life on this foundation.*
