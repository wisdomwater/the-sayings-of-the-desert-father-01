# Day 40 — A Pure Heart Toward Everyone

### Amma Sarah

Amma Sarah said that if she prayed for everyone to approve of her conduct, she would end up bowing at every person’s door. Instead, she chose a different prayer. She asked that her heart might remain pure toward everyone.

Her concern was not universal approval, because she knew that such a thing could never be secured. Different people would always see her differently, and their judgments could pull her in a hundred directions. What mattered was the condition of her own heart in the midst of those judgments.

### Devotional

There is a deep exhaustion in trying to be acceptable to everyone. We adjust our words, soften one opinion, emphasize another, and quietly monitor how we are being received. Over time, the attention of other people can become an invisible audience we carry everywhere.

Sarah sees the trap clearly. If her peace depended on everyone approving of her, she would spend her life trying to repair every misunderstanding and satisfy every critic. Her inner life would be governed by reactions she could never fully control.

So she changes the prayer. She does not ask God to make everyone understand her correctly. She asks for a pure heart toward everyone, including those who misunderstand her, disapprove of her, or judge her unfairly. That is a much deeper kind of freedom.

Purity of heart does not mean agreeing with everyone or pretending that every criticism is valid. It does not require abandoning conviction, boundaries, or honest disagreement. It means refusing to let another person’s opinion turn our heart toward resentment, contempt, manipulation, or the need to retaliate.

This is harder than winning approval. Approval can sometimes be gained through performance. We can learn what people expect and shape ourselves accordingly, at least for a while. Purity of heart cannot be manufactured in the same way because it asks what is happening beneath the performance.

We may behave politely while inwardly rehearsing arguments against someone. We may remain outwardly calm while secretly wishing they would be embarrassed, exposed, or proven wrong. Sarah directs attention to that quieter interior movement.

Her prayer gives us another way to live with disagreement. We can allow another person to misunderstand us without immediately rushing to correct their image of us. We can explain ourselves when explanation is useful, but we do not have to spend our lives managing every interpretation. There comes a point when we must release the outcome.

That release is not indifference. Sarah is not saying that relationships do not matter. She is saying that love cannot be built on the need to control another person’s response. A heart that requires approval will eventually become anxious, defensive, or manipulative.

A pure heart can remain open without demanding agreement. This becomes especially important when we are judged unfairly. The instinct is to build a case, gather allies, replay the situation, and imagine the moment when everyone finally recognizes that we were right. Even when some defense is necessary, the interior trial can continue long after the external situation has ended.

Sarah offers another measure of peace. The question becomes less about whether everyone understands us and more about whether we can still hold them without hatred. Can we disagree without needing to diminish them? Can we remain truthful without letting resentment become our companion?

There will be times when someone’s judgment hurts deeply. We may need to grieve it, confront it, or step away from a relationship that has become harmful. Purity of heart does not mean becoming unaffected by pain. It means refusing to let pain decide what kind of person we become.

This is a fitting place to end our movement through judgment and mercy. The Desert Fathers and Mothers have repeatedly turned us away from the courtroom we build around other people and back toward the condition of our own heart. Sarah now takes that movement one step further by releasing even the need to control the courtroom others may build around us.

We cannot determine every verdict spoken about us. We can only tend the heart from which we respond. Sarah’s prayer leaves us with the quieter work of remaining truthful, free, and open before God.

### Breath Prayer

**Breathing in:** *Purify my heart.*

**Breathing out:** *Keep me open toward everyone.*
